Pass385 · the cold
Vine dead · nobody

The cold came early and stayed. It took the harvest the whole length of the coast, and that's the famine, and everybody knows about that part. Here's our part. It took the vine. Black on the south wall first, then the north, the whole run down to the grafts. A vine is thirty passes coming back and nobody here has grown one up from nothing.

So this post is blind. If the water comes we'll know when we see it, the same as anybody standing on a wall.

And the sky-sound came through all of it. Every night of the cold, not a tone out of place. It's on the band and the band doesn't care what's growing. I sat in the dark and listened to it. The one thing here that has never been any use to anybody. It came perfect all winter.

There's no cycle name on the record above. The tablers didn't sit this pass. I've left it unreckoned rather than guess.

Bram died early in it. Hesk and I kept the tides between us after that. Then the cold took eleven of my feet in the second winter. Hesk read the rows out and I said them back, and neither of us could write. I want that put down plainly: we heard it and we lost it.

Then Hesk died.

Three passes with nothing in this book. Not a line. Not even a note of what came down and went unworked. I hadn't the feet to write down what I was failing to write down. I'm writing now.

The mark came down while I was empty, and it isn't in the presses, because nobody cut it, because there was nobody. So here's what I can see, and which part is guessing. Thirty-two is not sixty-four. I checked it three ways. Then it took the saying back, and after that thirty-two is not sixty-four again. So it can bend its own reading — for now this word means this other thing — do some work under that, then put the word back. I know that it does it. I don't have the mark that does it.

Everything after tonight is going to lean on the thing I lost. Not maybe. It went to the trouble of building it, so it means to use it, and every time it does I'll be reading a line with a word missing out of the middle.

Bram would have made one out of pegs to see it. I can't make one. I don't know its shape.

Pass392 · the offer

The water took Kell in the night. Over the bar and the length of the flat, with nothing behind it to stop. They're saying four hundred. It'll be more. The count is what got carried out, not what's under. Nobody on that flat was slow. They were asleep.

The net held. Not here. Our vine has been dead seven passes and this post felt nothing at all. Kell caught it. Low Ford caught it. They passed it on. The word came to me the way it comes to anybody, off the band, out of somebody else's watch.

So I hung out the signal on another station's warning. It went out an hour before, which is what the net is for. I stood on the wall and watched the lamps go along the coast, one, then the next, then the next. One of them was mine and it was borrowed.

Kell was where Ren showed them the short figures. A hundred and twenty passes ago. The head clerk gave her a dry coat. The steward came over on the ebb. The coast is putting money into the net and some of it comes here. She's putting this post back to two, paid out of the net money, from the spring. I said yes and thanked her.

Hesk kept this watch on half rations for two passes. She read the rows out to me when I couldn't write. She's been dead five passes.

Why it shook me more than the fork or the vessels did. This message has been exact since the first night. Every claim it makes, it makes all the way, and that's how my line learned to trust it: you check a thing and it comes true, and you check the next and it comes true.

Tonight it told me there's a kind of number you can't check that way. Not because I'm slow. Because it doesn't end. So it built , a mark for close enough to work with, and said how close. I don't like it, and I've written that down. I've checked it nine ways and it holds every time.

Those two things are both true and I'm leaving them both on the page.

One more thing about the endless figures, and I can't make anything of it. To write them down it changed how it writes a number. Every count it has sent since Ren has come in twos, place by place, and I have never once seen it write one another way. These come in tens.

It gains nothing by it. The row is no shorter. Nothing after it needs tens. My line has changed how it writes a number twice and both times to save work — Ren cut what hers was worth into the press, and what it bought was the adding done on the page. This one saves nothing.

I don't know why you'd bother. Down as a thing noticed and not a thing known.

Pass414 · the counter

Down to the table-house for their figure for the round-mark. I wanted to lay mine against theirs, and mine came off the wire. They have it to four places and three schools disagree at the fourth. I took all three.

While the girl was copying I put my two rows on the counter. The way it writes a count everywhere else, and the way it wrote these. I asked her nothing.

She looked at them upside down, the way clerks do. She said it's got two marks and it writes everything in twos. Then here it writes in tens, and nothing made it do that. So they've got ten feet.

There's no road from a number on a page to a foot. I'd not write it as knowing. She wasn't writing it at all. She said it the way you'd say somebody was tall, and went back to copying.

I have five. She'd no reason to think of it and I didn't say.

I checked it. It lands within the smallness it named, and if you take more terms it lands nearer.

One thing about the checking. Its way is to build each term whole from the bottom. For the sixth, lay one against two against three against four against five against six. Then go back to one and do it again for the seventh.

I didn't. Each term is the one before it, shared out by the next count. That's one working instead of five. By the fiftieth it's one instead of forty-nine. Same answer. Not the same work.

I took the short way without deciding to, the way you take the short way home. It only struck me writing this up that there were two ways and I'd picked one. Somewhere ahead is a saying where the two ways are a night and a lifetime. Whoever's holding this book had better know she's choosing.

Here's the part I keep going back to. Nobody at our end derived that. Nobody at our end has it at all. The round-mark we have, off a wheel, to four places, with three schools arguing the fourth. There is nothing on this coast to lay against this one and no wheel to get it off. It came off the wire tonight with its working shown, built out of parts my line already had in the press. Any fellow reading this book from the start could rebuild it in an evening. The three schools have been arguing their own figure for two hundred passes.

Less than nothing

It has made a count that shouldn't exist. Set a count against itself and you get a square, and a square is never less than none. Everybody knows that. Maren had it out of the noise thirty passes into her watch. Tonight it named a thing that, set against itself, gives less than none:

{{EX:minus-i}}

My first reaction, and I'm not proud of it: I thought the wire had been dirty and went and got the second copy. Same. The third. Same. It isn't an error. It's a thing it has decided to have, and it doesn't argue for it — sets it down like a peg and starts building on it, and everything on top comes out true, about ordinary counts I can check.

Then it laid down one line and I have looked at it every night since:

{{EX:float-exp-2}}

The endless figure from six passes ago. The round-figure the tablers have measured since before this post. The count that can't exist. Put together in one working. It comes out at less than none, and joined to one it comes out at none. Nothing in that line was invented for the line. Every piece of it arrived separately, over passes, doing other jobs. I have checked it as far as I can check it and I cannot check all of it. Here is my whole account, and then I'm going to bed.

Things I can show: the raising, the folding, the near-mark, the fifty terms, the squares.

Things I can't show: that the count that can't exist is allowed.

Things I lost: ten sayings, near enough, in the cold. One of them is the one everything since has leaned on.

Ona takes the watch at the turn. She's better at this than I am and she'll want to know what's underneath her. That's what the three lists are for. I have ruled a line across the rest of this page, corner to corner, so nothing can be put in after me.
