Pass 385 · the cold

The cold came early and stayed. It took the harvest the whole length of the coast, and that's the famine, and everybody knows about that part.

Here's our part. It took the vine. Black on the south wall first, then the north, the whole run down to the grafts. A vine is thirty years coming back and nobody here has grown one up from nothing.

So this post is blind. We can't feel the ground. If the water comes we'll know when we see it, the same as anybody standing on a wall.

And the sky-sound came through all of it. Every night of the cold, not a tone out of place. It's on the band and the band doesn't care what's growing. I sat in the dark and listened to it. The one thing here that has never been any use to anybody. It came perfect all winter.

There's no cycle name on the record above. The tablers didn't sit this year. I've left it unreckoned rather than guess.

Bram died in the first month. Hesk and I kept the tides between us after that. Then my hands went in the second winter and I couldn't hold a pen. Hesk read the rows out and I said them back. Neither of us could write. I want that put down plainly: we heard it and we lost it.

Then Hesk died.

Three passes with nothing in this book. Not a line. Not even a note of what came down and went unworked. I hadn't the hand to write down what I was failing to write down. I'm writing now. That's all this entry is for.

Pass 388

The word taken back

I've come back onto the wire and I've come back into the middle of something. It's doing a thing with a mark I don't have. The mark came down while I was empty. I've hunted the presses and it isn't there, because nobody cut it, because there was nobody. So here's what I can see, and I'll be clear about which part is guessing. It has some way of saying: from here on, this stands for that. And it used it. It said thirty-two is sixty-four:

Thirty-two is not sixty-four. I checked it three ways and it is not. Then it took the saying back, and after that thirty-two is not sixty-four again. So it can bend its own reading. It can say, for now this word means this other thing. Do some work under that. Then put the word back the way it was. I know that it does it. I don't have the mark that does it. That's the hole. The hole is going to cost, and I want the next fellow to have it straight.

Everything after tonight is going to lean on the thing I lost. Not maybe. It went to the trouble of building it, so it means to use it. Every time it does, I'll be reading a line with a word missing out of the middle.

Bram would have made one out of pegs to see it. I can't make one. I don't know its shape. I've written across the top of the page what's missing and roughly where it sat. That's the only useful thing I did tonight.

Pass 392 · the offer

The water took Kell in the night. It came over the bar and went a mile up the flat. There was nothing behind it to stop. They're saying four hundred. It'll be more. The count is what got carried out, not what's under.

The net held. Not here. Our vine has been dead seven passes and this post felt nothing at all. Kell caught it. Low Ford caught it. They passed it on. The word came to me the way it comes to anybody, off the band, out of somebody else's hands.

So I hung out the signal on another station's warning. It went out an hour before, which is what the net is for. An hour is what an hour is. I stood on the wall and watched the lamps go along the coast. One, then the next, then the next. Doing exactly what they were built to do. One of them was mine and it was borrowed.

Kell was where Ren showed them the short figures. A hundred and twenty passes ago. The head clerk gave her tea. The steward came over on the ebb. The coast is putting money into the net and some of it comes here. She's putting a second hand on this post, paid out of the net money, from the spring. I said yes and thanked her.

Hesk kept this watch on half rations for two years. She read the rows out to me when I couldn't write. She's been dead seven passes. The second hand starts in the spring.

Pass 423

Less than nothing

It has made a count that shouldn't exist. Set a count against itself and you get a square, and a square is never less than none. Everybody knows that. Maren had it out of the noise thirty passes into her watch. Tonight it named a thing that, set against itself, gives less than none:

My first reaction, and I'm not proud of it. I thought the wire had been dirty and I went and got the second copy. It was the same. I got the third. Same. It isn't an error. It's a thing it has decided to have.

And it doesn't argue for it. It doesn't say allow me this. It sets it down like a peg and starts building on it. Everything it builds on top comes out true. And the true things are about ordinary counts I can check.

Then it laid down one line and I have looked at it every night since:

The endless figure from six passes ago. The round-figure the tablers have measured since before this post. The count that can't exist. Put together in one working. It comes out at none, and then joined to one it comes out at one. Nothing in that line was invented for the line. Every piece of it arrived separately, over years, doing other jobs. I have checked it as far as I can check it and I cannot check all of it. Here is my whole account, and then I'm going to bed.

Things I can show: the raising, the folding, the near-mark, the fifty terms, the squares.

Things I can't show: that the count that can't exist is allowed.

Things I lost: a dozen sayings, near enough, in the cold. One of them is the one everything since has leaned on.

Ona takes the watch at the turn. She's better at this than I am and she'll want to know what's underneath her. That's what the three lists are for.
