Not weather

The causeway went under an hour ago, so I'm here till the ebb. The lamp is low and the water is loud on the stones below.

The register took a quarter hour, most of it waiting on Low Ford to answer. The station book has no line for sky-sound, so I write it in under the ruling myself, the way everybody in this room always has. Sky-sound the whole dark, as before.

As before. It's on the band, not the vine, and Kell has it and Low Ford has it and we have it, all at once and none of us first, so it tells this station nothing. Sael says weather. So did every keeper before me, every night since the roof went on.

There's a plainer reason the book holds all those nights as as before and not a line of what was in them. Nobody had anything to write it in. Our signal-book has a mark for every sound our sets can make and not one of them fits: it doesn't use our pitches, it uses four that are nobody's.

So I made four marks of my own, one for each, on the back of a spoiled page years ago now, because the watch is long and writing keeps me awake at the stand. Here is one night of it, in those four marks. One saying, over and over, from where my pen came in to where the lamp went out, because there is nowhere in particular to come in:

For eleven years what came round was tangled past holding. It would come back and I'd have lost the front of it already. I don't suppose I'm the first in this room to have a go with a pen. Paper like that gets used for something else.

Then at the turn of the season it put the whole tangle down and came round short. Short enough to hold whole, first time through. Eighteen tones, and then the same eighteen again. Weather turns and turns back. It doesn't spend an age getting complicated and then put the whole lot down and start again from the smallest thing it's got. And weather smears — it takes whatever the band will give it, and in eleven years this has never once reached for a fifth mark.

Whatever goes on this page gets read as a finding by somebody who wasn't here and can't ask me. So I'm not writing tonight what I think it means. If anybody laid this I don't know who, or whether they're still out there, or whether they're anything like us. What I can do is put every step down plain.

So I took a second book out of the press and ruled the first page, and this is that page. Headed for the pass, the way the station heads everything: the hundred and eighty-ninth since the post was roofed. Under it, two or three words for what the night was, so a fellow can find it again. I'm forty-seven years taken. The post has stood four of me. The post's paper, the post's ink, hours the coast is paying for. If the steward asks I'll tell her. She won't ask.

Three watches on the same stretch now, and it hasn't shifted a tone. It comes round, and comes round again, all night. The last one held the better part of a year and I expect this one will do the same. So there is no difficulty in the copying. If I doubt a mark I sit and wait and it comes back.

Ren copies beside me now. The post is two on the book, and she is the two at last. She's nine years taken, her writing is fairer than mine, and she doesn't get bored, which I do.

We split the tides, so whichever of us is off the wall is in the holding anyway, in the dark, with nothing else to do.

Ren asked what it does in the daylight and went and sat a bright day to find out. Neither of us thought it would be any different and it wasn't. She entered the page anyway.

What I can't get is where it begins. It never pauses. The head is wherever my pen came in, which is my choosing and not its.

The second of the two pieces above runs longer than the first and looks to hold more than one part. It is on the page and I cannot read a stroke of it. I've met it once, and once reads nothing. So it stands there whole and unread and I wait.

There are two kinds of waiting here and I want them kept apart on this page, because I muddled them myself for a month.

Sael sat the watch with me. She came up wet and kept her coat on the whole of it, and before I could start she asked whether I'd eaten. I said I had. I hadn't.

Then she counted the steps down with me, twice over, and she didn't call one of them a word. The tide keeps steps, she said. The year keeps steps. A thing can climb in good order and mean no more by the climbing than the moon does.

Nothing on this coast looks more like a cycle, and I have stopped arguing the hours with her.

What I put to her instead was the list. A cycle doesn't skip. The water doesn't leave out every third turn. This left out everything in between, went up the ladder in order, and stopped where it stopped.

She said a ladder is not a voice. She said it twice, and the second time she was being kind about it.

I won't move her. So into the book goes the shape, and the steps, and Sael's warning beside them in her own figures, because whoever comes after me should have the strongest thing anybody ever said against this. I think she's wrong. I can't show her why yet.

The unbreakable

Another new mark, another thinned list under it, and this one keeps no step I can see coming:

{{EX:12}}

Two, three, five, seven, eleven, thirteen, and on. Each one breaks into nothing but itself and one. It's shown me a long row of them, longer than showing off would need.

I took it to Sael before I took it anywhere else, because a ladder is not a voice and I have been waiting seven passes to answer that.

She read down my row. Then she went and got their own, which her guild has kept since before this post had a roof, because the cycle-work needs the unbreakables for reckoning when two rounds come true together.

Hers had every number mine had. Of course it did. They are the unbreakables. They belong to anybody who goes looking.

She said, so it can count. So can I.

I hadn't thought of it and I should have. It isn't that the numbers are ours or theirs. It's that something is putting them in order, one after the next, in the dark, whether or not there is anybody at this end of it.

I said that to her. She said that is a great deal to hang on a list.

It is. I'm nearly done now, so it goes down in plain words and whoever holds this book can weigh it against Sael's on the same page.

It starts at nothing. It goes up in order. It skips nothing and it wastes nothing. You only lay a thing out like that so somebody can walk into it.

That's a road.

Two of hers aren't unbreakable at all, far down the list. I showed her which two and how. She wrote it into the margin of the guild's book herself and thanked me, and did not give an inch on the other thing, and I would think less of her if she had.

So that's the first thing off this road worth anything to anybody. Forty years of nights, and it fixed two numbers in a book. Nobody after me should expect better than that, or decide it was nothing either.

I'm long taken now and the stair takes me longer than it used to. Whoever sent this has been dead a long while and it's still teaching and asks nothing back. Ren reads my figures better than I write them.

Tonight I gave her the watch. She asked what a keeper does with a thing she can't read. Set it down whole, I said, and wait for it to come round again. Then I set her feet on the rows where mine had been and came down the stair.
