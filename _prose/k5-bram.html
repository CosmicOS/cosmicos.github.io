The knot

Neru's gone. I was thirty-three passes at her side and the last four I read the rows out to her — I'd read a row and she'd say go on, or she'd say again. That was the whole of how I learned this, so I'm going to keep writing it the way I said it. If it sounds like somebody talking, that's why.

And it didn't give me the knot. It made one in front of me, out of Iso's makers:

Three of them, one inside the next. That's the whole of it. I wrote it out twice to be sure. A thing built out of what I already have is a different sort of gift from a thing that arrives whole.

And the two pulling-marks aren't new either. Look at them next to each other. Both are the knot-mark with a small thing hung on the end — a nothing on the one that pulls the first out, a one on the other. I'd been carrying them as two more marks to learn. They're one mark and a tail, and it stands plainest in the first line:

{{EX:knot-again}}

Right. So I went down to the bench and made one of my own. Two pegs and a cord. Either peg gets you the cord, the cord gets you the other peg. Then tie the second peg to a third with another cord and you can walk it. Peg, cord, peg, cord, peg. As long as you like.

Mine and its aren't the same thing. Its is three makers deep and every pull goes down through all three. Mine is a peg you pick up. They answer alike. I checked that twice, on nine of them.

Neru would have asked me what it's for. I don't know yet. I know it's a way to hold any number of things using a thing that only holds two. That's a good trick.

Nine pegs on the bench when I stopped. I've left them there. It was a good trick.

Pass354 · the boat

Our boat's rotten and the steward says there's nothing for a new one this cycle or next, so I'm doing it myself. Beached her in the cut, pulled six planks, scarfing in what I can get. Most of the pass on that, and no reading. I want it down that there was no reading, and why, so nobody after me thinks the wire went quiet.

Doss is dead nine passes and I do the tides alone. The steward has been asked twice to put somebody else up here. She says the post is two on the book. It's been one on the water since Doss.

And I walk the vine alone, which is the part I don't like. Neru taught me that run after she'd stopped being able to feel it. She had the words and I had the feet. I'd put mine on a graft and say quiet, and she'd ask quiet how, and I'd try to tell her, and she'd say that's stirring. Neither of us ever knew whether she was right.

So that's how I have it. Learned off somebody who couldn't check me. There's been nobody to say I'm wrong for nine passes, and I hang things out on it.

There's a thing in her book I found when I read the watch in. The eleventh, and the low field. I said stirring. She went down and put her own feet on the graft and wrote quiet, and put her name in the column to make it stand, and the water came over before light. She couldn't feel that graft. She says so on the same page.

She never told me. What she did was write down that she hadn't told me.

It's grazing and there was nobody on it, and I still don't know what I'd have done with it said out loud. What stays is that she put it where I'd get it, which is not the same as telling me.

Anyway. The boat floats. She's ugly. I sailed her round to Low Ford and back on the ebb and she took nothing, and sat in her a while at the mooring feeling pleased with myself. Then I came up and lit the lamp.
