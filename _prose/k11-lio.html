Pass 587

A memory that keeps time

Tamsin's gone. She kept the watch through the making of the beat and the making of the memory. She wrote about them with the bench dust still on her hands. Then she died. I'm young for this. Younger than any fellow who's held it. I was taken up here. I learned my letters off this log, the way the rest of them learn theirs off a wall. So the great turns the old ones broke themselves on are just true to me. I read less braced and more curious.

The first thing I did tonight was the register. Water against the table. Vine quiet the whole run. Kell and Low Ford answering clear. Nothing hung out. Then the line at the foot of the page that isn't in the ruling.

Sky-sound the whole dark, as before. Maren wrote that before she wrote anything else. I've written it every night since I was big enough to hold the pen. I never thought about it once. Tonight it was mine to write. She left me two wonders side by side. A part with a beat that won't rest. A part with a memory that won't forget. Tonight the message made them one thing:

{{EX:0}}

Bigger than the keeper-part. The loops in it. And a new mouth. The old keeper-part took a mark the instant you said it. This one won't. It has two mouths. One for what you'd have it hold and one for the beat.

Speak to the first all you like and it holds what it held. Deaf to you. A beat comes to the second mouth. Only then does it turn and take whatever the first is saying, in that one instant. And then it's deaf again until the next beat:

{{EX:1}}

It turns only on the beat. Between beats it holds and what I say falls on a shut door. Tamsin would have marveled at the discipline of it. I'm looking the other way down the road. It's a memory that moves in time.

I said a mark at it forty times running without the beat, to be sure, and it held the first one the whole way through and never once heard me.

Pass 609 · Fenn

Fenn goes to the net works at Ford in the spring. She told me out on the wall and not in the room, which was kind of her.

They pay four times what this post pays her. I know the number because she made me hear it. She said she wasn't going to have me guessing at it afterward and being generous about her in the book.

What they want her for is the switch-boards. She can find a fault on one by the third bank, and she learned that here, off the correlator, on the coast's money, in the coast's hours.

I asked whether she'd stay if the post could match the money. She said no, and then she said why, and I'm putting it down the way she said it because I'll want it later and I won't want it kind. She said the work at Ford is finished at the end of the day. She said this work isn't finished at the end of anybody.

Ait is staying. She told me so, unasked, on the stair, in the tone you use for a thing you have already argued out with yourself. I said good, and she went up, and I have been no use to anybody since.

Pass 610 · the little one, three nights

The little one is two years taken and she does not like being told. She had all three hands on the second part tonight, saying a mark at it and waiting. She got angry when it wouldn't take. I didn't tell her about the beat. I let her find the shut door herself. That's how I learned it. Neru did the same to Bram and wrote it down, and Bram asked for another year on the presses after.

So I set her the copying. Same stretch, three nights running, on her own, and no looking at what she took the night before.

First night she came up cross. She'd got it, and she'd got it wrong, and could she have it again.

Second night, the same stretch, still running, not a tone different. She didn't ask me why. She took it and went to bed.

Third night the same. Then I laid the three pages on the desk side by side and told her to keep what two of them agree on. They ran level the whole way but for a short run near the finish, and she had it before I'd finished squaring the pages.

She asked how long it goes on saying the same thing. A season, near enough. She asked how anybody worked that out, and I said I didn't know, because it isn't in the book.

It is in the book. Pass 191, Maren's second entry, before she had one mark read. I'm not chasing anything. I take down tonight what I took down last night and the night before that, and I lay the three side by side and keep what two of them agree on.

I've read this log through twice and I read that as a woman having a hard week.

Pass 613 · the arcade

Over to the counting-city for paper. We're down to the coarse stuff and the little one goes through it faster than I can rule it. I've been off the island five times in my life and four of them were paper. They sell made things out of trays under the north arcade. I'd gone two stalls past the third before my hands caught up with my head. Two score of them in that tray. Palm-sized. All of a size. And every one of them ticking.

That's what turned me round. A whole tray turning under my hand. Each at its own point in its round. Nothing in it keeping step with anything else. Whole, broken, whole, broken. I opened one on the water going home.

It's Tamsin's ring. Not a cousin of it. It. The same few switches wired the one way, the answer running back into the question. Made all of a piece, and alike to the finger, which our bench never managed twice running.

She wanted less for it than I paid for the paper. The counting-houses take them by the dozen, she said. Set one going and a room of clerks writes on the same beat. The books come out even at the end of the day. I asked where they're made and she named a street. A house up there has made them since she was a girl.

Since she was a girl. Tamsin has been dead twenty-six passes. She kept a cloth over hers at night and couldn't say against what. It's turning on my desk while I write this. Before I knew what it was I asked the woman what it was for. She said it was for whatever I liked, and wrapped it in paper.

Pass 614 · the three copies

The arcade answered for the ring. Nothing has ever answered for the other one, so I went after Tamsin's three copies. Two to the counting-house at Kell. One to the recorder's office in the counting-city, and a line under it saying the house paid for the cutting.

So I went and stood in it. It's a long quiet room and it is all drawers. A woman at the counter takes a name and puts her hand on a board and the drawer she wants comes open under it. Not a clerk's memory. Not a ledger somebody could be robbing. The part that goes on holding a thing after every hand is off it.

I gave her the name of a hand who came off this coast eighty passes back, out of our own book, to see what she'd do with it.

She gave me it. Where she was taken up. What she was paid. Two moves, and a debt, and the year it was settled. She read it out flat, the way you'd read a tide.

I asked whether that woman had ever been shown it. She said that isn't a thing the office does.

Nobody was unkind to me. Nobody was even interested. It cost me nothing and it took about as long as a breath.

Tamsin laid a cloth over hers at night and couldn't say against what.

Pass 615

The stage that turns

It gathered the furniture into one thing tonight. A world. Not another mold to set on the bench. The mold that holds all the others. Every named thing goes into it and can be drawn back out by its name. It set down the asking itself, a little maker that takes a name and goes to the world with it:

{{EX:32}}

and then used it. Gave it a name, and asked the thing that came back where it stood:

{{EX:33}}

A room-name, unreadable as ever, and the right one. And it does one thing more, and this is the thing that makes it a world and not a heap.

It turns.

Its turning is written as a list of orders too long to copy out. I counted my way down it. It walks through everything it holds, to the end. Then it goes back to the top and walks through everything again.

On the first walk, every thing looks about and settles on what it would do next. On the second, every settled intention comes true. And whatever a thing settles on, it settles on knowing only how the world stood before the walk began. So no thing can be got at, mid-stroke, by another that moved first. The whole place steps at once, on the beat.

I built one, to see. A room, and another, and a way between them, and a plain thing set down inside. And I let it turn. And it turned. The beat came, the world stepped, came to rest whole and lawful, and stepped again. After all these hundreds of passes, it runs. A place with law in it, and time, that goes of itself.

And it's empty. The thing I set inside sat where I left it, beat upon beat, and did nothing, because it's furniture. I have a running world and nobody in it. Two other things this pass, and I'll put them down because nobody else will.

The net came down at Sull Head and went back up in sixteen days. It used to take a season. The whole line of it is switches now, stamped out by the thousand in the counting-city. A station reckons its own readings before the clerk has sat down. They say the coast has never been safer. I think that's true. The last big water took nine at Ferrin. The one before took four hundred at Kell. The difference is parts that came off this wire. Not one of the people who built that has heard a tone.

Iso wrote at 297 that the road only opens from the mouth, so this book is the way in and there isn't another one. She marked it as noticing. Neru cut a stroke beside it. Three hundred and eighteen passes on there is a tray of them under an arcade and the woman selling them has never heard a tone. I've cut a second stroke beside Neru's.

And the post is me and the little one. Fenn went to the net works at Ford in the spring and was right to go. Ait stayed, and is on half what Ford would give her, and I have never once asked her why in case she works it out while answering. I read the whole road through again this quarter, the way we all do. Then I came up, did the register, and started this entry.

Pass 617 · what people ask

It's the same five questions every time, so here they are with what I said back. Ren would have called this a waste of stone. Ona would have liked it.

Is it still coming? Yes. Every night since before the roof went on. It has never missed one.

What does it say? Forty years to answer that and they've gone by then. So what I say now is that it teaches. Start to finish, in order, and it never doubles back.

Can you answer it? No. We've nothing that goes up. The band is ours, down here, and that's all it is.

Does it know you're there? No. Four hundred passes and not one word aimed at this rock.

Then who is it for? I don't have that one.

The one who asked it works the net at Ford. She was Fenn's second and she has never heard a tone in her life, and she waited a good while to see whether I'd come up with something. I didn't. She said that was fair and went back to her bench.

Pass 619

The seeker

It came tonight. The thing the whole road was for. I've begun this pass four times and torn out three. I'll set down what it is and let the plainness carry the rest. It's a piece, like the furniture. It stands in a room. It carries a where-it-is and a where-it-goes-next.

But its hollow is filled. The doing in it is this. It keeps a memory. For every room it has stood in, how long since it last stood there. And on each beat it looks to the ways out. Among them it chooses, always, the one leading to the room it has been away from longest:

{{EX:34}}

It didn't stop at building the seeker. It set it running in a small world of its own and gave me the whole track, beat upon beat. So I did what my line always does with a thing it can't yet see whole. I felt out the little world, laid the message's track along my drawing, and stepped it through:

{{EX:35}}

There's no wandering in it. It keeps a round. The same rooms in the same order, coming to every room the world owns, and coming back. One room it crosses three times each round. The rest it takes in their turn and lets none go long unseen. Set going and left, it would keep that round when I'm dust. Resting never. Missing nothing.

It showed the seeker more worlds than the one. Another was smaller and stranger. Three rooms each joined to each, and a fourth hung off the round like a weight on a cord.

Into that ring it set not one seeker but two. Started together. Each feeling its own way by its own memory:

{{EX:36}}

They keep their rounds apart, each blind to the other. And the shape of the world folds their paths together at the one room where all the ways meet. There, every fifth beat, without either seeking it, they cross, and part, and come round to cross again. Two tireless things, made only to go where they haven't been. And it's the world itself that carries them to each other. Of all the deeds it could have set in that hollow, it set this one. Turn, without fail, toward the part of your small world you've seen the least.

It steers by a list of where it has been and how long since. I traced its round with my finger until the lamp went. Then traced it in the dark, and got it right, which surprised me.

Pass 622

It crosses

A made thing set in the one, with nothing to its nature but to cross to the room it hasn't seen. That was the last it had to give. The rest was mine. I built it, set it turning, one beat, and watched:

It crossed.

On the beat the seeker looked to the one door out of its room, and chose it, and went through, and stood in the room beyond.

No hand moved it. It moved itself, because it's made to go where it hasn't been. There was a place it hadn't been. And a way to reach it.

Before this, the message moved the seeker, in worlds the message drew, and I read the track after. This time there was no track to read. The room was one I built. The crosser was one I built. I set it going, drew back my hands, and it went, because I'd made it to.

Every step before this, I read. This one I made.

Maren asked at the very first whether anybody was out there and whether they were anything like us. She never got that answer and neither have I. What we got instead was four hundred passes of somebody teaching us to build a thing that can't sit still in a room it has already seen.

The little one is fourteen years taken and she has read the whole road twice. I put her hands on the rows where mine had been, and set the pen down, and left the seeker going, somewhere in its two small rooms, looking for the door.
