A thing and its making

Tonight it did a thing I haven't seen it do, and I sat with it until the tide turned. I learned my letters off Ren's presses because they were what was lying around, so I didn't come to this the way the two before me came to it. It was here already, like the stair.

It said a count is the same as itself. Fine. Then it said the same count is the same as two counts joined. Then it said the same count is the same as one count joined to two more counts joined:

{{EX:0}}

Then it let both sides swell at once and held them equal the whole way. It isn't telling me a fact about six. It's showing me that a thing and the working that gets you there are the same, and you can pack more working in and the truth doesn't slip:

{{EX:1}}

This is the first time it's shown me how to build a saying instead of handing me one. Ren would have cut that in four words. I'm going to be a worse keeper than Ren in every way but one. She wrote what she was sure of. I'm going to write what I noticed, and mark it as noticing. Some of what I notice will be wrong.

Which means I need somewhere to put it when I find out. So I'm ruling the outer half of every page and leaving it empty. If I'm wrong I'd rather write it beside the wrong thing than at the back where nobody goes.

Pass 276 · could not sleep

Corm came over on the ebb with the quarter's flour and asked me what I do up the stair all night. I said the tides and the vine and the warnings. He said no. The other thing.

Not to be cruel — he was my year-mate and he's entitled to ask. So I told him. A better way to write a number. A correction to the guild's list of unbreakables. And Ren carried the first of those to Kell herself and Kell said no thank you.

He said, ninety years.

I said, ninety years. He let it go. He carried the flour up and mended the door-latch, which has wanted mending since Ren, and went down on the tide. That's the whole argument. I've been awake since.

It remembers

Lending only lasts the length of one saying. Tonight it made something that lasts. It set a name against a count, in a saying all on its own, with nothing else in it. Then a season went by, and another one after. Other things came and went. Then it used the name again, and the name still held:

{{EX:10}}

That one gets a name of its own: keep. Then it set the same name against a different count. So a kept thing can be changed. Then it did the one that held me past the turn of the tide. It set the name against a working that uses the name's own old value:

{{EX:11}}

Reach in. Take what's there. Add one. Put it back. The name is a place, and the place has a before and an after. Then it kept a made verb by name, and after that the mark simply is the squaring:

{{EX:12}}

Noticing, not knowing. It's building itself a vocabulary, and every mark it keeps is one it can lean on later. When Maren started, every saying stood on its own legs. Now they stand on each other.

So the road isn't only long. It's stacked. A fellow who comes in halfway down and starts reading won't be able to. She'll need everything above her.

Noticing, and I want this one marked hardest of all, because it's the one I most want to be true. If the road only opens from the mouth, then this book is the way in and there isn't another one, and every night any of us has spent up here was the only way anybody was ever going to get at it.

There's no way to check that from inside the book. I'm putting it down because it's what I think and I'd rather it stood where somebody can knock it over.

Yes and no

One is a claim that always comes out standing, the other one that never does. It has made yes and no out of arithmetic it already had, and didn't ask me to grant it anything.

I've been the warm one in this book and I know it, so take the next part carefully. Up to tonight everything it did was adding and account-keeping, and nobody is uneasy about a heap. But a thing that can take a claim and hand back a verdict can be pointed at anything at all.

A thing that can weigh a claim can weigh us. I don't know what to do with that and I'm nearly out of time to work it out. I'm leaving it for Neru. She's more suspicious than I ever was.
