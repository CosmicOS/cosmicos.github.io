[Pass ⣂ ⣽]  Not weather

on watch Maren, alone

The causeway went under an hour ago, so I'm here till the ebb. The lamp is low and the water is loud on the stones below.

The register took a quarter hour, most of it waiting on Low Ford to answer. The station book has no line for sky-sound, so I write it in under the ruling myself, the way everybody in this room always has. Sky-sound the whole dark, as before.

As before. It's on the band, not the vine. The band is ours, the posts' own, how we answer each other down the coast, and this comes in on it without being sent. Kell has it and Low Ford has it and we have it, all at once and none of us first, so it tells this station nothing. When the water stirs we know where it started by which post had it first, and this has no first. So there's no telling where it comes from, and nothing here to point with if there were. Sael says weather. So did every keeper before me, every night since the roof went on.

There's a plainer reason the book holds all those nights as as before and not a line of what was in them. Nobody had anything to write it in. Our signal-book has a mark for every sound our sets can make and not one of them fits: it doesn't use our pitches, it uses four that are nobody's.

So I made four marks of my own, one for each, on the back of a spoiled page passes ago now, because the watch is long and writing keeps me awake at the stand. Here is one night of it, in those four marks. One saying, over and over, from where my pen came in to where the lamp went out, because there is nowhere in particular to come in:

(what comes — and comes, and comes) «the raw stream, dense and unbroken, wrapping and fading out past the visible edge; it opens ˦˨˨˨˥˦˦˥˥˨˦˨˩˩˨˩˥˨˦˨˨˨˥˦˦˥˥˨˦˨˩˩˨˩˥˨˦˨˨˨˥˦˦˥˥˨˦˨˩˩˨˩˥˨˦˨˨˨˥˦…»

For eleven passes what came round was tangled past holding. It would come back and I'd have lost the front of it already. I don't suppose I'm the first in this room to have a go with a pen. Paper like that gets used for something else.

Then at the turn of the season it put the whole tangle down and came round short. Short enough to hold whole, first time through. Eighteen tones, and then the same eighteen again. I laid one round under the next and they stand over each other the whole way. It held to that the better part of a pass. Then it moved on, and the new one runs longer:

(a round)    ˨˦˨˩˩˨˩˥˨˦˨˨˨˥˦˦˥˥

(the next)    ˨˦˨˩˩˨˩˥˨˦˨˨˨˥˦˦˥˥

(and since)    ˨˦˨˩˩˨˩˥˨˦˨˦˨˨˩˩˩˩˥˨˦˨˨˩˩˩˨˥˥˦˦˥˥

So it started again from almost nothing, and it has been growing ever since. I've sat with that for a season.

Weather turns and turns back. It doesn't spend an age getting complicated and then put the whole lot down and start again from the smallest thing it's got. And weather smears — it takes whatever the band will give it, and in eleven passes this has never once reached for a fifth mark.

None of it can go in the station book. That page belongs to the coast and a clerk at Kell reads it. Anything I set down there gets read as a finding by somebody who wasn't here and can't ask me. So I took a second book out of the stores and ruled the first page, and this is that page. Headed for the pass, the way the station heads everything: ⣂⣽, the hundred and eighty-ninth since the post was roofed. Under it, two or three words for what the night was, so a fellow can find it again. I'm forty-seven passes taken.

I'm not writing tonight what I think it means. If anybody laid this I don't know who, or whether they're still out there, or whether they're anything like us. What I can do is put every step down plain. This is the post's paper, the post's ink, hours the coast is paying for. If the steward asks I'll tell her. She won't ask.

[Pass ⣂ ⣿ · later]  

Three watches on the same stretch now, and it hasn't shifted a tone. It comes round, and comes round again, all night. The last one held the better part of a pass and I expect this one will do the same. So there is no difficulty in the copying. If I doubt a mark I sit and wait and it comes back.

Ren copies beside me now. The post is two on the book, and she is the two at last. She's nine passes taken, her writing is fairer than mine, and she doesn't get bored, which I do.

We split the tides, so whichever of us is off the wall is in the shelter anyway, in the dark, with nothing else to do.

Ren asked what it does in the daylight and went and sat a bright day to find out. Neither of us thought it would be any different and it wasn't. She entered the page anyway.

What I can't get is where it begins. It never pauses. The head is wherever my pen came in, which is my choosing and not its.

The second of the two pieces above runs longer than the first and looks to hold more than one part. It is on the page and I cannot read a stroke of it. I've met it once, and once reads nothing. So it stands there whole and unread and I wait.

There are two kinds of waiting here and I want them kept apart on this page, because I muddled them myself for a season.

The near one is most of a pass. I sat the short round out, from the day it came to the day it moved, so I have that one measured. It is still saying this and it will be saying it tomorrow, so a poor copy is nothing to be afraid of. I sit down again and take it again. That gets me a true copy. It does not get me a reading.

The far one is longer and I hold it less firmly. This thing put down everything it had and started over from its own beginning, with me sat in front of it, so whatever I can't read now it will come round to saying again. I have that from the one turn, and no more. Not tomorrow. Ren asked how long and I said I don't know, and I don't, and I've done the arithmetic twice since and I don't think it falls in her watch or in mine.

Sael sat the watch with me. She came up wet and kept her coat on the whole of it, and before I could start she asked whether I'd eaten. I said I had. I hadn't.

Then she counted the steps down with me, twice over, and she didn't call one of them a word. The tide keeps steps, she said. The pass keeps steps. A thing can climb in good order and mean no more by the climbing than the moon does.

Nothing on this coast looks more like a cycle, and I have stopped arguing the hours with her.

What I put to her instead was the list. A cycle doesn't skip. The water doesn't leave out every third turn. This left out everything in between, went up the ladder in order, and stopped where it stopped.

She said a ladder is not a voice. She said it twice, and the second time she was being kind about it.

Then she asked me where it sits. Her guild keeps a place on the sky for everything that runs to a cycle, and she wanted the one for this. I told her there isn't one, and why: every post has it in the same moment, so there's no first to reckon from. She wrote that down. Then she said a thing with no place on the sky is not on the sky, and she'd sooner look for it in the band itself, or in our own sets.

I won't move her. So into the book goes the shape, and the steps, and Sael's warning beside them in her own figures, because whoever comes after me should have the strongest thing anybody ever said against this. I think she's wrong. I can't show her why yet.
