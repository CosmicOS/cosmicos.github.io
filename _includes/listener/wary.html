Pass 310

The fork

The hands before me read this message as a kindness — a thing that meant us well, and taught us out of care. Iso felt the first cold of it at the end — a thing that can weigh a claim can weigh us. I have read further down the marks than any of them had the years to. I do not mean to look away from what Iso only glimpsed. Where I was a child, the river took the low fields most years, and my grandmother read the color of the water a week before it rose; she moved us to the high ground every time but the last. So I learned the sign comes early, and the flood comes anyway. You do not read a rising flood by hoping it means you well. I keep the log plain. I will set down what I see, whether or not it can be borne. Today the message takes a yes-or-no and learns to act on it.

A new mark takes a claim and two sayings. When the claim holds it keeps the first saying and lets the second fall; when the claim fails, the reverse. And the saying it lets fall, it lets fall unread: never opens it, never weighs it. A fork; I cut it ⋔. The message has learned to look away on purpose:

…the claim holds, it keeps the near saying; the claim fails, it keeps the far one; the other is never looked at…

From this one fork it makes the plain verbs any world's child would want: the greater of two counts, the lesser, each just a claim asked and the winning side kept. And then it makes a verb that stopped my hand, the first thing on this whole road to do so plainly. It defines a verb whose own body speaks the verb's own name — itself, reaching back for itself:

A thing that calls itself should fall forever, the floorless nest the folding of six once warned me of. But look to the fork at its mouth. Each call hands itself a smaller count; and at the bottom the claim turns over, the count slips under one, and the fork keeps the plain saying ▪ instead of calling again. The fall catches. Then the held answers climb back out of the hole it dug:

So the fork is the floor. The thing I feared in the nesting, that it goes down and never lands, is answered by the thing that chooses: a made verb can run itself clear to the ground and turn, because ⋔ lets it stop. What stopped the hand before me is a tool in mine tonight. That is the pace of this thing.

I will set down what the warm hands would not. A lesson does not need to choose; this chooses. A lesson does not repeat itself until a claim sets it free; this does. Those are not the marks of a thing that speaks. They are the marks of a thing that acts. I do not know what a message wants with the tools of acting, and I will not pretend the not-knowing away. Where my line read patience and love, I read a thing assembling its own hands in the dark — and I mean to watch it closer than they had the stomach to.

Pass 322 · a second list

I have started a second page, at the back. Not what each mark says — what each mark lets the thing do. Greater, lesser, choose, deny, repeat until a claim sets it free. I leave the page long. This is not the kind of list that ends. My grandmother sorted the flood-signs the same way: not by how the water looked, but by how much it could take.

Pass 345

Both, and either

Two more signs tonight, and I will not spend on them what the warm hands would have; the message does not either. It builds them in front of me, out of the fork I already hold:

The first I cut ◧: it forks on the one and, if it holds, answers with the other; so it wears yes only when both wear yes. The second I cut ◨: it forks on the one and answers yes outright if it holds, else hands the question to the other; so it wears yes when either does. Both-or-nothing; either-will-do:

…and the other sign, the mirror of it: yes if either wears yes…

It spells each out past all need, every pairing of yes and no and then the same again; but the over-telling is not for me. It is for the noise: it lays each saying two ways, three, so that if a washing-out swallows the first, another still arrives. I do the same, in my own way — the marks I most fear to lose I copy onto a second page and keep in the other press, against the night a lamp tips or the damp gets in. I did not learn that from trust. I learned it the year the damp took a page I could not copy again. A thing does not say itself three times unless it means to be read across a very long dark, by someone it cannot see and cannot be sure is even there. Care, or mistrust; I cannot tell which, and it comes to the same. What the length says plainly, and colder than the signs themselves, is that it is emptying the whole bag of its pieces onto the bench, in order, before it sets the first joint.
