Pass 310

The fork

The three before me read this message as a kindness — a thing that meant us well, and taught us out of care. Iso felt the first cold of it at the end — a thing that can weigh a claim can weigh us. I have read further down the marks than any of them had the years to. I do not mean to look away from what Iso only glimpsed. Where I was a child, the river took the low fields most years, and my grandmother knew the water by its carry a week before it rose; she moved us to the high ground every time but the last. So I learned the sign comes early, and the flood comes anyway. You do not read a rising flood by hoping it means you well. I keep the log plain. I will set down what I see, whether or not it can be borne. Today the message takes a yes-or-no and learns to act on it.

A new mark takes a claim and two sayings. When the claim holds it keeps the first saying and lets the second fall; when the claim fails, the reverse. And the saying it lets fall, it lets fall unread: never opens it, never weighs it. The message has learned to look away on purpose:

…the claim holds, it keeps the near saying; the claim fails, it keeps the far one; the other is never looked at…

That mark I cut fork. From this one fork it makes the plain verbs any world's child would want: the greater of two counts, the lesser, each just a claim asked and the winning side kept. And then it makes a verb that stopped my hand, the first thing on this whole road to do so plainly. It defines a verb whose own body speaks the verb's own name — itself, reaching back for itself:

A thing that calls itself should fall forever, the floorless nest the folding of six once warned me of. But look to the fork at its mouth. Each call hands itself a smaller count; and at the bottom the claim turns over, the count slips under one, and the fork keeps the plain saying ▪ instead of calling again. The fall catches. Then the held answers climb back out of the hole it dug:

So the fork is the floor. The thing I feared in the nesting, that it goes down and never lands, is answered by the thing that chooses: a made verb can run itself clear to the ground and turn, because lets it stop. What stopped her is a tool in mine tonight. That is the pace of this thing.

I will set down what they would not. Tonight it chose. It repeated itself until a claim let it stop. It looked away, on purpose, from a saying it had decided not to open. Three doings, and not one of them is a thing you do when you are telling somebody something. Where my line read patience, I read a thing assembling its own hands in the dark.

Pass 316 · what it takes

The sickness came in on the summer boats. It went through the coast in a season and it is not finished. It does not kill many. It takes the hands. Up the coast they have started calling it the numbing.

It starts in the fingertips and works back. There is no pain in it worth the name, which is why nobody stops in time. You keep at your trade, and one morning you cannot tell a cut line from the grain of the board it is cut in.

Three in the house have it. It has my off hand entire and has started on the two I read with. I hear the tones as well as I ever did — it does not touch the ear. I can no longer read the founder's rows, and I cut a fair line still, and cannot check it by feel.

So I have set the girl Bram to re-cutting the oldest presses: deep and blunt, four strokes where Maren cut nine, the fine strokes that told one kin-sign from its neighbor run together into one broad one. It will read worse. It will read. What we cut this year the line reads for four hundred passes; what Maren cut, nobody reads again. She is quick and she has all three of hers, and I am standing over her while I can still tell whether she is right.

When my right goes I will have her read it back to me aloud, and I will listen. I told her so tonight. She said she would not mind.

Pass 322 · a second list

I have started a second page, at the back, in the deep cut. Not what each mark says — what each mark lets the thing do. Greater, lesser, choose, deny, repeat until a claim sets it free. I leave the page long. This is not the kind of list that ends. My grandmother sorted the flood-signs the same way: not by how the water looked, but by how much it could take.

Pass 345

Both, and either

Two more signs tonight, and I will not spend on them what the warm ones would have; the message does not either. It builds them in front of me, out of the fork I already hold:

The first I cut both: it forks on the one and, if it holds, answers with the other; so it wears yes only when both wear yes. The second I cut either: it forks on the one and answers yes outright if it holds, else hands the question to the other; so it wears yes when either does. Both-or-nothing; either-will-do:

…and the other sign, the mirror of it: yes if either wears yes…

Two more after them, and it did not build these as new things at all. It made them out of what it already had: not less is either-of-two — greater, or the same — and not more the mirror of it. Read the second line and you can see there is nothing in it but the pair I cut tonight and the marks the founder pulled out of the noise:

It could have handed those down as two more bare marks and I would have taken them. It did not. It showed them being made, out of older stock, in the open, where I could see there was nothing in the making I had not already been given. I set that down as a habit of the thing and not as a kindness to me.

copied, not read
Sixty-odd further postures of both and either, every pairing of yes and no and then the pairings again with the counting-marks standing in for the plain yeses. Copied entire. I read to the twentieth and could see where it was going.

The over-telling is not for me. It is for the noise: each saying laid two ways, three, so that if a washing-out swallows the first, another still arrives. I do the same — the marks I most fear to lose I copy onto a second page and keep in the other press. I learned that the year the damp took a page I could not copy again. It is emptying the whole bag onto the bench, in order, before it sets the first joint.
