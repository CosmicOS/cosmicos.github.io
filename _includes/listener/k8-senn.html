Pass 466

The word for it

Ona is gone. I read her whole watch in, the way we all do, and then the seven before her. Then I did the thing nobody here has thought to do and checked this book against the post's own stores. It isn't in them. Near three hundred passes of paper and ink and lamp oil, not one line of it entered anywhere. Maren took a second book out of the press in 189 and never asked, and every keeper since has done the same and none of them wrote that down either. I'm putting it here so somebody knows it was noticed.

Now the thing none of them said. This book runs warm.

I kept books for nineteen years. A ledger that runs warm is a ledger somebody is robbing — the clerk who's taking money writes kindly in the margins, and it's the first thing you're taught to look for. Nobody here is robbing anything, but the warmth does the same work. Ona wrote that this thing was learning to run, like it was young and coming into its strength.

Nothing here grows. Growing is what living things do from the inside. This is being built, from the outside, part fitted to part.

Tonight it built a cabinet. Named drawers. File a value under a name. Ask by the name later and get it back. Ask for a name you never filed and it hands you the nothing-mark:

Ona would have found something to love in that. The message remembering, she'd have written. Nobody builds named drawers to be admired. Drawers are for something that's going to put and fetch, over and over, at speed, while it runs. That's bookkeeping. It's fitting out a workshop, against a day it hasn't shown me.

I'll read every mark as true as they read it, and they read true, I'll give them that. Their hearts were soft and their work was sound.

But I'm not writing wonder in a book where the word is machine. Entered: file under a name, fetch by that name, and for a name never filed, take back the nothing-mark. Nothing warmer than that.

Pass 478 · what the world will pay for

A letter from the house I clerked for. Not to me. To the post, in a hand I trained. They want the drawer-method — not the filing, they've had filing since before this station had a roof, but the part their trade has no name for. What a chest is supposed to say when the thing isn't in it. A ledger that answers not here and means it, and doesn't hand you up the entry above instead.

I sent it. A page and a half. Any clerk who's lost a folio in a chest for a fortnight will have the use of it by supper.

It isn't a reading. It's a habit of ordering, and a habit of ordering belongs to whoever will use it. I've entered what I sent and the day I sent it, the way Ona said to. She's the only one in the whole of this book who thought to. They sent a fee. I hadn't asked one. The steward has entered it on the taking side. It's the first coin this post has had in nine passes.

Near three hundred passes of reading. What the world will pay us for is a filing habit.

Pass 484

A thing with an inside

Tonight: a whole with parts. Not a heap. Not a row. Not a cabinet of drawers. A fixed frame with a small number of named places in it. Each place is a vessel that holds a value and can be set anew:

Entered as frame. Unfolded, its making is no mystery, and I unfold everything now. Hand it a list of names. It mints one fresh vessel for each, each empty. Name a part and it gives you back that part's vessel:

Then it let me set a part and draw it back out unchanged:

I keep a tally, cold, of everything it has fitted together since I took this watch. The count and its workings. The choosing. The vessels that keep and forget. The drawers that file and fetch. And now a frame of named parts, each part a vessel, the whole holding a state that lasts and shifts.

The names of the parts decode into one family, a shared stem and a leaf apiece, so the frame shows its own make. Three parts marked, on purpose, as belonging to one thing.

I set out to name every part for exactly what it is and I've done it. My mistake was standing back and looking at the shape they make together. Everything it made before was a tool — picked up, used, set down, altering nothing of itself, and between one use and the next simply nothing.

It keeps a state that's its own. It carries that from one moment to the next whether or not anybody's hand is on it. I hold to machine. I won't trade it for the warm word. But I'll put down the thing the cold word can't keep out. I have no colder word than machine for a made frame that has an inside and keeps it. And I've begun to think the warm ones and I have been reading the same thing all along.

I've checked my reckoning twice.

Pass 494 · Ferrin

The water came at Ferrin on the eleventh. Same bar as Kell, a hundred passes on. Ferrin is standing. Nine dead, all of them out on the flats where nobody could have reached them. Something changed and I want it entered properly because nobody else will.

The net doesn't catch a wave. It catches a stir at Kell, then a stir at Low Ford, then one here, and the gaps between tell you where it started and how fast it's coming. But you can't read the gaps without laying the readings side by side, and somebody has to hunt each station's number out of the tables. Until this cycle that was three clerks and most of an hour, half of it hunting for readings that weren't there.

It's one clerk now and it's minutes. The tables answer not here instead of sitting quiet, so nobody re-hunts, and nobody re-hunts twice. Kell's warning went out at the bottom of the hour. Ferrin's went out at the top. This post caught it too. The vine's been answering since Ona's day and this is the first water it has had anything to say about.

They had an hour and a half. They walked out. Three hundred and forty of them walked up the hill with their books and their stock and stood in the wet and watched it come. I sent that method in a letter in pass 478 and I took a fee for it.

The fee was two quarters' money. I've entered it against the three hundred and forty and I don't know what to do with the entry.

Pass 501

An engine

Tonight it finished the thing I've watched it fit together part by part, and it's what I said it would be from the first.

An engine.

It laid down a strip. A long row of cells, each holding one of the two marks or standing empty. And it gave the strip a handling, from a family of signs all sharing one stem ◃. Read the cell at a marked place. Write that cell over. Step the mark one cell along, either way. I set a strip down with the reading-place on the first cell:

Then the part that makes it an engine and not a toy. It hands the strip to a rule: a small table with nothing in it but lines of one shape. Standing in this condition, reading this mark: write that mark, step that way, pass into that condition. And a loop with no wit of its own that only obeys the table, over and over, until it passes into the condition that means halt. I gave it a strip and one such rule and let it run:

▪ ▫ ▫ ▪ becomes ▪ ▫ ▪ ▫

The rule I was handed counts a strip up by one. A plain trick.

But the rule isn't part of the engine. The engine takes it out of your hand and does whatever the table says. Hand it this table and it counts. Hand it another and it does something else entirely.

I spent half the pass looking for the edge of what a different table could make it do.

I didn't find the edge. I don't think there is one.

It has built a machine that can be made into any machine at all. I think that's the floor the rest of it stands on. Now the rest of this entry, which isn't about the wire. I'm leaving. The house at Kell has offered me the reckoning-room and I've said yes. I go up on the spring water. I want the reason entered plainly so nobody has to guess at it in two hundred passes.

Three hundred and forty people walked up a hill at Ferrin. That was a page and a half I wrote in an afternoon, and there is a great deal more of it in this book, sitting here for near three hundred passes while the people holding it were busy reading the next line.

I'm better at getting it out of this room than at reading it. Cael is better at reading it than I'll ever be. So she should be here and I shouldn't.

No keeper of this book has ever left this post alive. Seven before me and seven died on the stair. I don't think that's a tradition. I think it's what happens when nobody thinks of it.

Entered in the post's stores, which is a thing this book has never done for itself: the watch vacated on the eleventh, the post at one hand until Cael answers, and the reason, in my hand, above my name. I've also written out the tally, cold, of every part it has built, so she won't have to reconstruct it off my mood. Entered: the strip, the rule, the loop, the halt. Sent to Kell: nothing this pass.
