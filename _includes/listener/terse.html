Pass 232

Same, and more, and fewer

The keeper before me is dead. I take the watch. ◇ brings three new marks in a short season. The first heads a long run of pairs:

…and on, the same, more times than I will copy…

Their sense comes quick. The first heads two counts that match; I cut it ⬠. The other two head counts that differ — ◒ where the first is the fewer, ◓ where it is the more. So: same, fewer, more. Until now the message told us only what is so. Now it sets one count against another and says which is the greater. That is a new kind of thing for it to do. The plainest of the three, same, is the one it spends the most marks to be sure of.

Pass 239

No

One new mark. It keeps odd company. It stands only before claims I can already see are false, never before a true one:

…and so, again and again: the true thing said plain, the two false ones denied…

It says: not this. Until now every line the message sent was a thing it held true; this is the first mark it owns for what is false. I cut it ⬢. A small sign. It does a great deal.

Pass 246

Heaps joined

There is fighting in the streets below the station, over grain. It climbs nearer the watch each pass. The message does not mark it. Tonight it joins heaps. ◇ brings a new mark. It heads two counts, and swears their whole ⬠-equal to a third named beside them:

…and more of the same, each true when I count it…

The named count is always the two others put into one heap: the new mark joins them, and I cut it ⬡. Two heaps become one, greater than either. Every mark before this told me a thing. This one makes a thing. That is the difference, and it is a large one.

Pass 253

Taken from

The low quarter burned three nights back. The smoke finds the ventpipes and sets my lamp guttering as I write. The message does not slow. ◇ brings a new mark, in the join's own posture: two counts, and a third sworn ⬠-equal beside them. But the third comes out the smaller, not the greater:

…and on, always true when I count it…

A heap taken from. The first holds the more; the second is drawn out of it; the rest stands. This is no new act. It is ⬡ the join walking backward, and I cut it ⬓: ⬓ unmakes what ⬡ made. A thing and its undoing, shown in one breath. I set the mark down, went up to judge how near the fires had come, and came back to the next.

Pass 260

Heaps of heaps

◇ brings a new mark. Beneath it, a long season of triples. It heads two counts and names a third — not their join, not their remainder, but something larger. Every pairing, in turn:

…every count against every count, more than I will copy…

Heaps of equal heaps: one count taken as many times as the other says, poured into one. I cut it ⬔. An old face returns. A count laid against itself gives back the thinned list — the ◈-squares — that cost an early keeper a season of wrong guesses. ●●● against ●●● is nine. What one hand puzzled over, a later hand reads off a plainer rule. That is what the log is for.

Pass 267

Short figures

It tires of my long runs of marks, I think — or knows I do. It stops spending them. For each pile it now sets down a short figure of the two lone tones, and swears it ⬠-equal to the pile I hold:

…up to that same height, a digit shy of full, and past it — a fistful of marks shrunk each time to a handful of tones…

I know these marks. They are the two signs I cut when I first cracked the cups. The long strings I set aside then as names — a label, not counted with, I wrote — were number all along, packed close. It has folded its long piles into the very tone-strings it showed me at the start. Only now do I see the two were one.

Then it says the join and the heaps again, no longer in marks laid one to a unit but in these packed figures. Each holds when I unfold it the long way:

For a long night the rule escaped me — why these three tones should weigh a five and not a three. Then I stopped reading each figure across, and set the short ones in a column, the least on top, and read down:

▪one
▪▫two
▪▪three
▪▫▫four
▪▫▪five

And there it was, in the columns. The tone at the right hand turns with every step down — there, gone, there, gone — the mark of a one, present in every second count. The tone beside it turns half as often, holding two steps before it flips: worth two. The next, half as often again: worth four. Every place is double the one to its right, climbing away to the left. So ▪▫▪ is a four and a one, the two between them left dark — five; and ▪▫▫ a four alone. The mark I had waited for never came, because none was needed: the rule was folded into the order of the marks all along, and I had only to read down instead of across. I am old now. The runs are long. I will not copy many more.
