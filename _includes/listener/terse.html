Pass 232

Same, and more, and fewer

I cut her name in the doorstone, and I cut the record above it — the watch changing hands, plain, where it cannot be mistook or lost. No keeper before me set it down so. But I am a cutter. I trust a thing cut over a thing said. The log is Maren's no longer. It is mine, and it will say less. What I am sure of, I set down. What I am not, I leave blank. A blank waits. A wrong mark stays.

◇ brings three new marks in a short season. The first heads a long run of pairs:

…and on, the same, more times than I will copy…

Their sense comes quick. The first heads two counts that match. I cut it same. The next two head counts that differ — one marked where the first is fewer, the other where it is more. Same, fewer, more. Before this, the message told us what is so. Now it weighs one count against another. That is new. And same it guards hardest. The plainest thing. It spends the most marks to be sure of it.

read back
Two counts that match, I mark same; where they lean, fewer or more.
Pass 239

No

One new mark. It keeps odd company. It stands before false claims only. Never before a true one:

…and so, again and again: the true thing said plain, the two false ones denied…

It says: not this. Every line before was a thing held true. This is the first mark it owns for the false. I cut it deny. A small sign. It does a great deal.

read back
The truth I say plain; the false I mark deny.
Pass 246

Heaps joined

Fighting in the streets below, over grain. It climbs nearer the watch each pass. I bar the door. I keep the lamp low. The message pays it no mind. Tonight it joins heaps. A new mark heads two counts and swears their whole ⬠-equal to a third named beside:

…and more of the same, each true when I count it…

The named count is the two others put in one heap. Always. The new mark joins them. I cut it join. It gives the join both ways round — the two counts, then the two swapped — and swears each the same heap. The sum does not care which I set first. Two heaps become one, greater than either. Every mark before told me a thing. This one makes a thing.

read back
Two heaps poured into one, and it minds not which I set first: join.

The join's posture again. Two counts, a third sworn ⬠-equal. But the third comes out smaller, not greater:

A heap taken from. It is ⬡ walking backward. What the join makes, this unmakes. No long entry on it. The low quarter burned this week. I went up once, to judge how near. Then back to the marks. Maren would not have gone up at all.

Pass 260

Heaps of heaps

A new mark. Beneath it, a long season of triples. It heads two counts and names a third. Not their join. Not their remainder. Something larger. Every pairing, in turn:

…every count against every count, more than I will copy…

Heaps of equal heaps. One count taken as many times as the other says, poured into one. An old thing comes back. A count laid against itself gives back Maren's thinned list — the ◈-squares, that she read in her first year. ●●● against ●●● is nine. She puzzled a season over it. I read it straight off a plainer rule. That is what the log is for.

Pass 267

Short figures

It tires of my long runs of marks. Or knows I do. It stops spending them. For each pile it sets a short figure of the two lone tones. It swears the figure ⬠-equal to the pile I hold:

…up to that same height, a digit shy of full, and past it — a fistful of marks shrunk each time to a handful of tones…

I know these marks. Maren cut them, cracking the cups. The long strings she set aside as names — a label worn, not counted with, she wrote — were number all along. Packed close. It has folded its long piles into the very tone-strings it showed us at the start. Names and numbers. The same thing. I see it only now.

Then it says the join and the heaps again. No longer one mark to a unit. In these packed figures. Each holds when I unfold it the long way:

For a long night the rule escaped me. Three tones weighed a five, not a three. I did not see why. Then I stopped reading across. I set the short figures in a column, least on top. I read down:

Every place double the one to its right, climbing left. So ▪▫▪ is a four and a one, the middle dark. Five. And ▪▫▫ a four alone. The mark I waited for never came. None was needed. The rule was in the order of the marks all along. I had only to read down, not across.

I am old now. The runs are long. I will not copy many more. To the hand that comes after: set it down whole, and read down before across. The first counsel is Maren's. The second is mine.
