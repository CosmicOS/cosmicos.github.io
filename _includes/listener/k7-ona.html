A plainer cut

Vess is gone. She left me three lists and an apology she didn't owe me. I'm going to write to you the way she wrote to me, straight at whoever's holding this. Tonight it wrote a maker a plainer way. It used to make a two-slot verb by nesting one-slot verbs and feeding them one at a time. That works and it's ugly to read. Now it puts the slots side by side in one go:

read back
The doing it could always shape, only said shorter — one breath where it used to spend many: .

Nothing new can be done with it. Everything it does, the old nesting did. Notice that, because I nearly walked past it: this thing has been running four hundred passes and it just went back and tidied up its own writing. Now the other thing, and it isn't off the wire. The steward came up with the quarter's money and a docket from Kell. The numbers on the docket are written short.

Not spelled out. Not their old way. A scatter of marks to a count, place by place. Exactly the way this book has written them since pass 267.

That's Ren, who carried a fair sheet to Kell herself and was given tea and no thank you. I asked the steward's clerk how long the houses have written that way. She said since before she was taken up, and looked at me like I'd asked what color the sea is. A hundred and sixty passes. From Ren's fair sheet to a docket, and nobody wrote down when, and Ren's name isn't on it and won't be.

So here's my advice to you, and it's the only advice in this entry. WHEN YOU SEND SOMETHING OUT, WRITE DOWN WHAT YOU SENT AND THE DAY YOU SENT IT. Nobody in this line has ever done that. I'm starting.

Not for the credit. So the next fellow can tell what went out of this room from what came into it, which is a thing nobody can work out afterward and everybody assumes somebody did.

Nothing but the doing

This one took me three nights and I still went to bed unsure. It built yes and no again — it already has yes and no, cut out of the sameness-mark by Iso a hundred and thirty passes ago. These new ones have no arithmetic in them at all. Each is a maker with two slots that gives back one of the two things you put in. The first gives back the first, the second the second:

That's it. That's the whole of them. No counts. No sameness. Nothing but a slot and a choosing. Then it did the counts the same way:

None is a doing that takes a doing and doesn't use it. One uses it once. Two uses it twice. Three would use it three times, and you can see where that goes without being told. The number isn't in there anywhere. The number is HOW MANY TIMES IT DOES THE THING. I checked them against the ordinary counts as far as I could push them and they behave.

Everything in this book stands on the counts. Maren found them in the noise and the whole road since is built off them, and I've always taken them for the floor. They aren't the floor. You can make them out of pure doing. A slot and a body and nothing else, and you get the same answers.

Pass438 · the ink

The oak-ink has gone. Not short. Gone. The galls failed two years running up the whole coast and the last of ours went into pass 434. I've been on lamp-black since, and it's poor stuff — it sits on the page instead of going in, and rub it wet and it's gone. So I do the reading in lamp-black and cut the marks that matter into the press after. Twice the work and half of what I used to get through.

Write this where you'll see it. LAMP-BLACK ENTRIES ARE PASSES 434 TO NOW. If they've faded by your time, the press has them. Go to the press.

A truth that keeps itself

It said none is less than none joined to one. True. It said three is less than three joined to one. True.

It could do that all night and I'd have entered it and gone to bed. Then it stopped doing it and said the thing itself:

…and so for every count I set into it…

I've named it every. Before, it gave me claims one at a time and I checked them one at a time. Now it gives me one claim that covers all of them, and there are more of them than there is time. And it isn't bluffing, which it proved by turning round and denying one:

A count is not always less than itself doubled. None isn't. So the every-claim fails, and it says so, and it's right. Then the other side of it. Not ALL but AT LEAST ONE. There is a count that is two set against itself. There is no count that is itself joined to two:

My line has been checking things off a list since Maren. Copy it, check it, enter it. You can't check off a list — there's no end to the list. So either it's asking me to take its word, which it has not done once in four hundred passes, or there's a way to be sure of a thing without checking every case of it. I don't have that way. I spent three watches looking and I'm entering that I looked.

And here's a thing nobody in this line has put on a page, so I will. Some of it is dull. Not hard. DULL. The eightieth proving of a thing you had at the sixth is dull. I have caught myself copying with my eyes shut and my feet going. That is worse than not copying, because it looks the same afterward. So I've started stopping at ten and entering the count. If that loses us something, it's mine and I'll wear it.

So here's a job for you, and I know I'll never find out whether you did it. Somewhere down this road it is going to show you how. When it does, go back and cut it in at the head of this pass, deep and big, where a fellow hunting for it will walk into it. I've left the space ruled.

The endless, caught

It has an if-then now. Not the fork, which chooses. This one is a claim in its own right. If the first holds then the second holds. The only way it fails is the first holding and the second not:

That last line is the one to sit with. If the first fails the whole thing stands, whatever the second does, which looks wrong until you try to write it another way and find you can't. Then it asked whether a thing stands in a row:

Call it in. And it didn't make it new. It built out of Vess's hunting-mark: hunt the row, and if what comes back isn't the nothing-mark, it's in there:

Vess wrote that everything after would lean on what she'd lost. Here's one leaning on a part she DID get, and I'd have liked to give her this. Then it went from rows to heaps. A heap doesn't care about order or how many times you say a thing, and it built the whole idea out of one question asked over and over. Is everything in this one also in that one.

And then sameness for heaps, and it's beautiful and it's two lines. Two heaps are the same heap when each is inside the other. Not by comparing them. By asking the containing question both ways:

Now the part I've been three passes on. It named a heap with no end to it. Every count there is. And it didn't list them, because it can't, and neither can I. It caught the whole endless thing in two claims:

…and so I ask it, and ask it — is this one in? and this? — and it is, and it is, and it does not stop…

None is in it. And EVERY count in it has the next count in it too.

Read those two together and the heap is pinned. None is in. So one is in. So two is in. It doesn't stop, and it doesn't have to be written out, because the rule does the walking. That's the answer to what I couldn't work out seven passes ago. You can be sure of an endless thing. Not by checking it. By building it out of a start and a step.

I left you a job at the head of that pass. I've gone and done it myself. Leave the space ruled anyway. There'll be another one.

Then it went straight on and did the even counts the same way, off the back of it:

I've read that line four times. The heap of all counts, the there-is, the both-mark Neru cut, and heaping. Nothing in it I wasn't given.

The walking of a map

It made a map. Places, and the ways between them, and nothing else:

Four places. Three ways. One to two, two to three, one to four. I pegged it out on the board before I read another line, because a thing with places in it belongs on a board and not down a page:

Then it asked whether you can get from one place to another in a single step:

One to two, yes. One to three, no, because you'd have to go through two and that's two steps. Then the same question asked a second way. The first way answers yes or no. The second gives back the step itself, so you come away holding the way and not only the verdict:

One to two, and it gives me the step. Two to four, and there's nothing straight between them, so it says no. This is our own trade coming back at us off the sky. The net is a map. Stations, and which can reach which. Low Ford has never reached us direct; what Low Ford has comes through Kell, and through another station on the way.

So when I want to know whether a thing is real I'm asking whether there's a way from there to here, and how many stations it came through. My house has been asking that since before this post had a roof.

Pass458 · the blank

I've found what Vess lost. Not the sayings — those are gone until the road comes round, and the round is longer than any of us get. But it used the bending-mark again this pass, and restored what it had bent, and to restore it it had to NAME it. The name is in the clear, and it's a name I have nowhere else in this book. The pass that brought it in is one of her three blanks.

So I can't read what she lost. I can tell you what it's called and where it sat and that it exists. I've cut it into the press at the head of the gap. The note beside it says this is a name and not a reading. The other thing I did was go back through her ledger.

Vess kept a ledger whenever she had the feet for it. She cut hers at 388, and the first three rows of it are the winter. A pass each, and nothing after the number but the words: nothing taken down.

Ren's form is cut in the press and copied at the front of the book, and I'd gone past it a hundred times taking it for a title. Three rows of nothing, in a form made a hundred and fifty passes before Vess was taken up.

She went back and cut those rows afterward. Which means she counted them. She couldn't hold the pen at the time. That's what a blank means here. She hadn't the feet to write down what she was failing to write down. Hesk was dead. There was nobody else in the room.

She apologized to me in her last entry. I've written under it that there was nothing to apologize for and that I've filled what could be filled.

She won't read it.

First, and after

A short one, and don't skip it. Three doings in a line. Do these in order and give me back the last:

It goes in the book as in-order. Set the vessel to eighty-eight. Then set it to six. Then ask it. Six. If those three had happened any other way round the answer would be different. The mark exists to say they don't. Bram found the vessels and wrote that arithmetic doesn't care when you do it. She was right and she stopped there.

Here's the rest of it, a hundred passes on. Once you have things that can be changed, you need a way to say THIS, AND THEN THIS. And is it, and it's four marks long, and it's the plainest thing on the whole road.
