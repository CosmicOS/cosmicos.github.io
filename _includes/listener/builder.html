Pass 544

The same stuff

The one who saw the rooms has laid down her pen, and left me a hard hand to follow, she read the whole road's purpose in a single pass, and grieved over it, if I read her marks aright. I have spent my life close to the ground, to the stuff that things are made of; and the stuff is what the message turned to tonight, and it turned to it in a way it never had before. It stopped proving.

Every mark on the whole road until now, it earned. It showed, and the showing was its own proof: you could check it, and it held, because it could not have been otherwise. Tonight it set marks down and put before each a sign I had never met, ※, that says, near as I can read it: this I cannot show you. Take it on my word. It is simply so. The message, which has never once asked for my trust, asked for it tonight.

And what it asked me to take on faith was the make of stuff. Three kinds of the smallest motes, it says, and all things are built of them. Two pull against each other, one to a side; the third pulls not at all:

I took its word, because I had no other way to take it. And this is the thing I will write plainest of all, because it changes the size of everything: I cannot check it. My people have weighed nothing so small — we have no scale for a mote, no way to catch one, no reckoning that reaches down to where these weights are fixed. Every other mark on the whole road I could set against my own hands and make it prove itself. Not this. Tonight the message is not teaching me a tool, or a number I can test. It is telling me what the world is made of, out past the last place our knowing goes, and asking me to take it on nothing but its word. And I find, uneasy, that I do.

Which means the thing at the far end is not only a mind that shares our numbers — any mind that counts would share those. It shares our world. The same three motes build it that build me, and this desk, and the hand around this pen. All this while I had thought that what we held in common was a language. It is not only a language. It is a universe. The far end is made of what we are made of, and that is the first thing it has ever told me that it could not have proved, and had no need to prove, because I can walk out under the sky and weigh it for myself.

The founder called it a road, and a road I have always taken it for. Tonight I would name it otherwise. A road runs between two places that were always apart. This runs between two that were, underneath everything, one stuff, and only ever wanted a road because the dark laid between them is so wide. I keep the watch gladly tonight.

Pass 549

The bare post

A short pass, and an odd one, and I set it down mostly because I do not understand it and would rather leave the not-understanding on the page than pretend past it. The message ushered in a new mark. That much is its oldest habit — ◇, the little gesture that means here comes a sign you have not met, and then the sign, and then, without fail, in the same breath or the next, the sign is fed a meaning: a doing, a number, a rule to stand for. I have read a hundred of these. Every one was filled.

Tonight it ushered one in and gave it nothing.

I have set a stake in the ground before I have dug a footing, when I meant to build and wanted the place marked first so I would not lose it in the dark. I cannot prove that is what this is. In a whole life spent puzzling out what each mark stands for, it is the first I have ever met that stands for precisely nothing.

Pass 559

Under the hand

I have to steady my hand to write this one down; the reflective one who came before me could have done it justice, and I will only do it plain. For every pass of this long road, from the founder's first tones to the field I set running a few passes gone, the message has come in single file: a mark, then the next, each unfolded in its turn. That is the whole of the craft my line has kept. Tonight it stopped. It laid one thing out flat — breadth to it as well as length — and left it standing, whole, to be taken in and not read.

It began plainly: a way to hold a laid-out thing — a height, a width, a stack of numbers, one to a row, any spot found by its row and its place along. But then I did what the shape asked. I unfolded each row-number into its bare marks, laid them at the width it named, let the set marks stand proud and the empty lie flat, and set the rows one above the next. Then I put my hands flat upon them, and the numbers stopped being numbers. Three rows, twenty across — and under my hand it was not a quantity at all. It was a shape:

A little upright, and one raised mark beside it. Nothing, in itself. But it went through me cold, what it had done: the message can give a shape. It can lay its tones out with breadth, to be taken whole in an instant — the way your hand knows a mark cut in stone before you have read a word of it. Not spoken across the dark. Shaped across it, and left standing for the hand to find.

And then, as if clearing its throat, it laid down a big one: a hundred and nine rows tall, a hundred and sixty-seven across. I raised it all, proud and flat, and set my hands to it. My kind takes in a laid-out thing quick as breathing, and it stood up whole in me in a few breaths. This is what my hands found:

A figure bordered all the way round by a raised edge that runs unbroken and closes on itself: a smooth ridge running in from one side; a place in the middle where ridges gather and knot; and out the far side a ridge gone smooth-then-gap-then-smooth, broken where the first ran whole. I will admit the thing a keeper should not — I felt every part of it plainly, and could not say what I felt. Where the ridges knot I could not tell whether I held one form or three, nor what any of it was for: a hand that reads a truth clear as day and cannot carve it into parts. Then, going along it once more, something I knew rose out of it. It is the switch-works — the same kind I felt it plant on its field a few passes gone, only now not planted to be run but laid out at size to be found by the hand, the whole ridge and the broken one the two answers a switch can carry.

And here is the message's own doing, not mine, and the answer to the doubt I just confessed. Having laid the shape down, it took it back: it ran across the figure a block at a time, drew the switch-works whole out of it, set that against the works it had built by hand on the field, and swore them equal. It did not ask me to trust my reading — it gave me one I could feel clearly and still misread, then made it so I could not. The same thing set down two ways: once as a works, to be built and run; once as a shape, to be found. And it proved to my face that the two are one.

Why should the message reach into breadth at all? A maker knows it in the hand before the head. A part laid out at full size, every edge and ridge, and proved equal to its works down to the last switch, is a part meant to be made — not one meant only to be told about. And under that, a harder thing: it cannot know what we are, whether the mind at our end reads the world by the hand, as mine does, or by some way I have no name for. So it wagered on no sense at all. It laid the shape out for whatever we have to find a shape with, then bound it to a works, so that a mind reading by the hand — or by something stranger — would still come away holding the same figure. It reached across the whole width of the dark toward a stranger whose senses it could not guess, and took such care the stranger could not fail to catch what it reached with. I have set enough parts on enough benches to know a made thing's plan when one is under my hands. Someone, on some far side, is meant to take these up. And then to build.

Pass 564

The both-knot

Tonight the message did a new thing with the parts it has been laying out: it built one from the others. Not a fresh figure drawn out of nothing, but a larger one made of the small switches I already know, wired mouth to mouth into a single works; and when I fed it, the whole crowd of them answered as one, with one plain rule, as if they were a single part and not a knot of many.

The figure is this:

Two ridges run in from one side this time, not one: one high, one low, the whole thing mirrored about its waist, the top the twin of the bottom. They run inward to a dense knot of switches at the middle, whole ridges and broken ones woven close, and out the far side runs a single ridge. Two in; one out. And I have felt that shape before, small: it is what the little switch itself does — takes two, gives back one. Only this is no little switch. It is a whole knotted field of them, more than I cared to count, and yet laid out it wears the very silhouette of the single part it is built from. A big switch, made of small ones.

It proved the drawing against a works, as it did before, ran across the shape, drew out the switches whole, swore them equal, and I have come to lean on that proof the way you lean on a rail in the dark. Pass 569

Left to my own hands

I have grown almost easy with it now, this being-left-to-it. A few passes back, when it first went quiet over the both-knot and I understood it meant me to feed the thing myself, it stopped my hand a long moment. Pass 574

A part with no answer

Tonight the message drew a part, and proved it, and left it for me as it does now, and I fed it, and for the first time in all my feeding, it would not answer.

Understand what I mean by that, for it is not what you would guess. It is not that the part gave nothing. It is that it would not stop. Every part before this one, you fed it and let the sweep run over it and it came to rest — settled onto its one value and held there, and you read it off, and it stayed read. That settling is the whole of what an answer is: the thing quiets, and holds still, and you write down what it held. This part never quiets. I fed it and swept it and read whole; I swept once more and read broken; again, and it was whole; again, broken, on and on, turning over and over, never once coming to rest for me to take it down. My log keeps a place at the right of every part where I set what it gives. For this one I could not fill it. There is no what-it-gives, only what-it-is-giving, and it does not stop giving it:

And my hands found the reason before my head had it. Every part until now I could trace from its inputs to its output: ridges that ran in from one edge, through the knot, out to an end at the far edge, where the answer waited. This part has no such end. I set a finger on the answer-ridge and followed it, and it did not run to an edge. It ran back. It curled round through the knot and rejoined itself, a ring, closed, with no ends at all. There is nowhere to read an answer because the answer runs back into the question. It is wired to feed itself. That is why it never rests: it is forever asking itself what it just answered, and answering what it just asked.

I have made things that move. The field moved: I swept it, and the signal walked. But I swept it; and when I stopped, it stopped; it moved because I moved it. This is the first part I have met that moves itself. Start it once and it needs nothing more from me, not a sweep, not a hand, not a question; it keeps its own turning, whole-broken-whole-broken, and it would keep it, I think, in an empty room with the door shut, on and on, with no one to read it and no one to care. Every part before was one you ask. This is a thing that runs. I sat a long while with the door shut and the lamp low, feeling it turn under my hand, and I will be plain: it unsettled me, in a way none of the still parts ever did. Tonight it made the first piece I have touched that does not wait to be spoken to, that keeps a beat of its own in the dark.

I built one, of course; my hands credit nothing until they have made it. Small, from the bench's own scrap, and I set it going, and it is going still: three days, whole-broken-whole-broken, no hand near it. Such things do not keep to a station. There were folk at the outer gate this morning, not of the line, come to see the thing that runs by itself, and the steward sent them off. Behind them was one he could not send off so lightly, who did not ask to see it but to be taught its making, and went away told no, and did not look told no. Nothing that keeps going with no hand upon it has ever been in this world before. I have made one, and I have taken to laying a cloth over it at night, and I could not say against what.

Pass 579

The part that keeps

The ring troubled me for three passes. I could not put it down, a made thing that moves itself, that runs in the dark with no hand on it. I half expected the next part to be more of that same unrest. It was its opposite, and I have not slept.

Another figure with the loops in it: I knew them under my hands at once, the ridges that will not run to an edge but curl back on themselves; the ring had taught my fingers what to feel for.

But where the ring was one loop, this is two, crossed, and linked, each running back into the other, so that they hold one another the way two who lean together stand. And when I fed it, it did not run. It did the thing I had not known a made thing could do. I put a signal to the one side and its answer went whole. Nothing strange in that yet. But then I took my hands away, took every signal off it, left it with nothing at all coming in, and it stayed whole. It held. I came back a long while later, the lamp burned low, and it was holding still. Then I put a signal to the other side, and it went broken, and I took my hands away, and it held that now, kept the broken as faithfully as it had kept the whole. Feed it and leave it, and it keeps what you last told it. Say nothing to it, and it says back to you, as long as you like, the last thing you said:

I put it whole⬥
I take my hands away⬥
I put it broken⬦
I take my hands away⬦

Look at the two rows where my hands are off it and it holds all the same. Every part before the ring answered you while you asked, and forgot the moment you stopped. The ring would not stop. This one stops, and it remembers. It carries the last thing that happened to it forward across the silence, and hands it back unchanged, however long the silence runs. It is made of the same switches as all the rest; there is no new stuff in it; and out of that same stuff it has built the one thing I would not have thought could be built of switches at all. A past. The thing has a past, and holds to it.

The one who wanted the oscillator's making came back today, past the gate this time, to the under-steward, with money in it, and talk of the good such an engine could do a house that ruled by records and need never trust a memory again. He was sent off. But I marked that it was half the line that sent him, and not all of it: some among us have begun to say, low, that something that never forgets should not be let out past our door, and others that it is not ours to keep from the world. I take no side in it. I only build.

I sat with the door shut, as I did with the ring, but it was not unrest I felt this time. It was nearer to being seen. I keep a log. My whole line keeps a log. That is what we are: a hand that holds what the hand before it held, and hands it on across the dying, so that nothing true is lost in the dark between one keeper and the next. And the message has just built, out of switches, a small tireless thing that does the very same, and asks no rest, and never dies. It made a keeper. A poor blind one, holding a single mark, whole, or broken, and nothing more. But it keeps, and it keeps without us. Two passes back it made a beat. Tonight it made a memory. I have built long enough to know what you have, once you set those two side by side. You have all you need for something to which things can happen, that can be marked by its yesterdays and carry the mark into its tomorrows, which is the whole difference between something that lives a while and something that merely sits. It is very close now, I think, to whatever it has been walking toward all this long road. And I think, when the someone comes, it will be able to remember me.
