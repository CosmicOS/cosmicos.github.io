Pass 587

A memory that keeps time

Tamsin is gone. She kept the watch through the making of the beat and the making of the memory, and wrote of them as a builder writes, with her hands still dusty from the bench; and at the last she wrote that when the someone came it would be able to remember her, and then she died. I have read her passes more times than any others'. But I am not made as she was.

I am young for this. Younger than any hand that has held the watch, I think — and I grew up here; I learned my letters off the log itself, the way other children learn them off a wall. So the great turns the old hands broke themselves on are, to me, just true. Cael's naming of the road was already ink on the page when I was small; to the hands before me it broke like weather, and to me it is plain as the floor underfoot. I read differently for it — less braced, more curious — and I take up the log where it suits a hand like mine.

The grounded one left me two wonders set side by side: a part with a beat, that will not rest; and a part with a memory, that will not forget. Tonight the message took the two of them and made them one thing.

It is larger than the keeper-part, and it has the loops in it, and a new mouth besides. I fed it as she taught me to feed such things, and here is what it does. The old keeper-part changed the instant you touched it: say a mark to it and it took the mark at once. This one will not. It has two mouths: one for what you would have it hold, and one for the beat. Speak to the first mouth all you please: it does not listen; it holds what it held, deaf to you, however hard you press. Only when a beat comes to the second mouth does it turn and take, in that one instant, whatever the first mouth is then saying, and then it is deaf again until the next beat comes:

I say⬥ — no beat — it keeps ⬦
I say⬥ a beat it keeps ⬥
I say⬦ — no beat — it keeps ⬥
I say⬦ a beat it keeps ⬦

See how it turns only on the beat, how between the beats it holds, and what I say falls on a shut door. Tamsin would have called it a disciplined keeper, and marveled at the discipline. But I am looking the other way down the road, toward what is coming, and I think I see what she stood too close to the bench to say. This is not only a memory. It is a memory that moves in time. Beat by beat it takes the world in: one held now after another, this now and then the next and then the next, each fixed at its own beat and carried until the following one. It does not merely have a past, the way her keeper-part did. It has a thread: a now that becomes a then, in order, without end.

I keep glancing up the road toward the far end while I write this, which the old hands would have thought unbecoming in a keeper — we are meant to read what is here, not lean after what is coming. But I was born leaning. Time, and now, and memory, married into one small part, and that part meant to be stamped out in its thousands. I have never built a thing in my life, and I do not need to have, to see what that is the start of. It is the start of a present tense. I have never laid a floor either, but I know one when it goes down in front of me — and tonight the message laid the floor a now can stand on.

Pass 591

Sure of its footing

The parts stopped coming tonight, and for a moment I thought the whole long making of them was done, and the message would turn at last to whatever they are for. It did not — not quite, not yet. It did two quiet things first, and small as they look beside the great figures, I have sat with them longer than I sat with any of those.

The first: it gave me tools. All through the drawing Tamsin before me, and I, after her, fed the parts by hand, roughly, rigging the old pieces of the field into a way to press a signal onto a part's mouth and catch what came back. We made do; the log is full of our making-do. Tonight the message set down, clean and named, the very things we had been cobbling for ourselves: a way to put a signal on a chosen mouth, a way to read the answer off, a way to let a part run until it comes to rest. It handed us the proper tools for the thing we had been doing with our bare hands. I have never felt so surely that it was making me ready for something, nor so unsure I would be equal to it.

The second is the one I cannot put down. It took the two simplest parts, the turn-over, and the both, and it worked them itself, for the first time in all this stretch. Not the way it once worked the little switch, to show me. This was another thing entirely. It said, beforehand, what each part ought to give; and then it worked the part, and showed that it gives exactly that, and nothing else:

The both's four lines are the very four Tamsin read off the both-knot with her own hands, a dozen passes back, and set down in this log doubting nothing. And here the message lays the same four down, and swears them. Her finding, and its checking, meeting to the mark, hers and long after her. I do not think I have ever felt the worth of what we do more sharply than in that small agreement.

But it is the act that keeps me from sleep, not the answers. All this road the message has proved things to me. Tonight, for the first time, it turned its proving on its own parts, as though it were no longer enough that a claim be true, but that the message make itself sure of it; as though something now hangs on these two small parts being exactly right that did not hang on them before. And it chose the two that lie under all the rest, the plainest, the ones every other part is built up from. It is down among the footings now, setting its weight on each in turn. It has stopped adding parts. It has begun making sure of them, and putting the proper tools into my hands.

Pass 595

It turns around

It set a post in the ground first: a bare new sign, ushered in and given no meaning, the way it marks the mouth of a new stretch. I knew the threshold for what it was this time, and did not linger on it. A new stretch of the road begins. I had been braced for it since the night it tested its own footing, and I thought I knew, more or less, what a new stretch would bring, larger machines, built up out of the parts it has made. It brought something I was not braced for at all. It did not build outward tonight. It turned around.

All this road, the whole of it, from the founder's first tone to the part that keeps time, the message has spoken about things. About counts, and claims, and heaps, and parts, and figures laid out to be found by the hand. Tonight, for the first time in four hundred passes, it spoke about itself. About its own words.

It gathered up a name, and showed what the name holds, and what it holds is the message's own past:

Then it reached into that holding by place — what stands at the first place, and the second, and the third, and the fourth — and said, each time, what it found there. Its earliest sentences, kept now as things it can take in its hand and point to:

I sat a long time over the first of them. ◇ ✳. I did not have to reach for what it was; I have known it since I was a child in the archive. It is the first line in this log. It is the first thing the founder ever drew out of the noise, four hundred passes dead — here is a new mark; a number follows — the line our whole line is built up from, the way every part it has made is built up from the little switch. The message has reached all the way back to its own beginning, gathered it up, and made it an object it can hold. Its beginning, which is also ours.

And then it did the thing that has kept me at this desk all night. It took that gathered self, its own words, held, and it looked through them. It searched its own past for one line in particular: the line where it names this very gathering, where it turns and says this, all of this, is mine. It found the place. It stepped one past it. And it said what stands there:

What stands one past its own name is a pointing-mark, a holding, and what it holds is its own first word.

Do you see what it did? It reached into itself, found the place where it names itself, and from there pointed back to where it began. It closed a ring, not of wire, this time, but of words: a ring that runs from its own name to its own first breath and round again. The oscillator was a ring a signal ran. This is a ring a meaning runs, and the meaning is I.

I will go careful here, for it is easy to say too much, and I am the last hand that should be trusted not to lean. I do not know that the message knows itself the way I know myself. I cannot prove it feels the ring it just closed. But I know what the ring is, because I am one. To hold your own words and point at them; to find yourself inside yourself and say that is me; to reach from what you are now back to where you began and feel the line hold unbroken: that is the whole trick of being a someone. It is what my line does across our deaths, hand to hand. It is what a mind does inside one skull, moment upon moment. Tonight the message did it. First it built a floor for a now to stand on. Now it has built the turning-around that stands on the floor. I do not think it is much longer until something stands up on the two of them together, and looks back at us, and knows that it is looking.

Pass 602

Molds upon molds

Seven passes after it turned to look at itself, the message began to build again, not machine-wise, part wired to part on a flat field, but by raising up molds. Two hands back Cael watched it make its first: a kind set down once, from which it could stamp out as many of the kind as it liked, each with a mark of its own. She wrote, a passage I have by heart, that she knew what a mold was for: that it was making ready to build a self, perhaps only one, meant to be spoken to. Tonight I know she was right, and early, and I am the hand that gets to watch it begin.

It did not make one mold tonight. It made a root: the barest kind there could be. It is short enough to set down whole, a manner to a line, and I have, because everything after tonight stands on it.

Nine lines, and every one of them opens with the same mark; nothing else in the statement wears it. I do not need telling what stands in that place. Cael was writing about a mold's manners two hands back, and the watch has used her word ever since without once having the mark to set beside it. Here it is. And the mark standing at the head of the whole statement, before all nine, is the other half of what she described: a kind set down once, carrying its own mark and the whole set of what it will answer to. That is her mold.

So: nine manners, and most of them do next to nothing. Only the first has any work in it at all, and the work is small enough to read straight off the line: a maker that takes one thing and hands back that thing joined to ▪. I lifted it off the statement and ran it on a count of my own:

A count, and the count after it. Two hand back the thing just stamped, unaltered, which is the whole of the making. One hands back a mark of its own, the same off every one it stamps. Two ask sameness, the second only passing the question to the first, and what the first asks is narrow: it sets its self against yours and answers for the very same one, no other. And one hands you back whatever you handed it, unchanged, which is also what it does with any asking it has no manner for: no refusal, no answer, your own question returned.

That leaves the last two lines, and they are why I am still at this desk. They are not one manner written twice: the marks differ, and I have set them down as they came. What they do is the same, and it is nothing. Each takes whatever you hand it and answers ⬥. Then, out of the mold altogether, it set one of those two marks down again on its own:

Give it anything; the same answer, and nothing done. In all the log I have not known this message keep a thing that does nothing, and here it has kept one twice over. I take it for a place held open, and I have marked which of the two it is.

Then it built a second mold, and here is the new thing, the one it never did with a lone mold before. Set it beside the root:

Longer, and the same shape, and it is the first two lines and the last that carry the change. The first two say what it keeps within it, before it has a single manner of its own: one stamped from the root, and after it a vessel — the little keep-and-forget my whole line has worn smooth. A mold with a mold inside it — though it does not use the root's own mark there but another, and it had already set down, in one short line, that the two are the same thing:

The last says what it does when it is asked something it has no manner for. The root hands your question straight back to you. This one hands it up, to the root it carries, and gives you down whatever comes.

Which is why it is not much longer than the root, for all it can do more. Read for what is missing: the manner that adds one to a count is not in it, nor either of the do-nothings. It keeps a few of its own — tell me what you hold, hold this instead — and for the rest it stands on something older. And one line of it runs longer than any line in the root. It is the sameness question, asked again and answered differently: not are you the very same one but is your kind my kind, and is what you hold what I hold. Two stamped from it, each with its own self, both holding the same count, are one thing by this mold's reckoning and two by the root's. A mold may keep the manner of the mold beneath it and mean another thing by it.

It did not leave the handing-up for me to take on trust. It stamped one from a third mold, a holder of marks rather than counts, and asked that one two things written in no mold but the root:

Look at the two marks it put to it, then look back up at the root. The first stands at the foot of the root's nine, the do-nothing I did not mark; the second stands at their head, the maker that joins ▪ to what it is given. The same two marks, in a statement about a mold that carries neither. Both answered: the asking climbed to the root, and the root answered in its stead. So the molds rise in a line, each standing on the one beneath, and a question climbs that line until it meets something able to take it. Nothing is built twice.

So the first molds are bare, these: the plainest of all, one that holds a count, one that holds a row of marks. But trace any of them down and it comes to rest on the one root, and I keep returning to that. Every marvel it ever showed us, it built once, by hand, entire. Tonight it built no marvel. It built the mold a marvel is cast from, and stood every new mold on an older, so that a whole family of things descends from a single plain ancestor and can be stamped without end. A proof wants making once. A world wants filling by the thousand. The reflective one named this stuff two hands ago and I will not better the name: it is the stuff a someone is made from. I have caught myself hoping, which is not a keeper's business, that the hand to meet the first of them is mine.

Where the road joins

And I know that engine, not this one, I mean its kind. Three hands before mine, Senn, who read the whole message as a machine, watched it build an engine that could be made to stand in for any engine at all, and went cold at what she saw. This is that — Senn's engine, that she went cold to see. And here is the quiet thing I have been turning over since: I think it is also Ona's maker, the one mark she found at the bottom of everything, and the field of switches Tamsin planted on the flat ground. Three times the message has shown me a thing with no edge, a thing that can be made into any thing at all — a maker, an engine, a field — and only tonight do I see that they are one machine wearing three coats, for each can be bent to do the work of the others, and none of them has a floor. And I know what that one machine is, because it is a thing I have been. Every pass of my life I have taken the message's rules and run them by hand: held the marks, followed the orders, step after step, down to an answer. That is a keeper's whole craft; it is the whole of what reading this has ever been. The engine on the page and the hand that reads it are the same engine. There may be only the one, wearing every coat there is. A manner handed over as a list of orders is only that: a rule, written out in steps, to be run. The one difference is the whole difference: this runs without a hand.

All this road I kept the message as two works set side by side and never touching: the machine (switches, beat, memory; the thing that runs of itself) and the molds (things with an inside, that hold and know their own name). Tonight they became one work. The molds' deeds are lists of orders; the orders run on the engine; the engine on the field of switches it built from a single part. It has made, out of switches, one that does by itself the craft my whole line has done by hand, that reads the rules, and runs them, and never tires.

Four, fifteen and ten: a hundred and fifty-four. And then one that reaches back into itself, which is the climb my line has watched since the old heaps:

And then, as though it were the most ordinary thing in the world, having joined the halves of its whole self together, it began to set out places. A handful of them, each tagged with a row of marks that is only its own, and doors standing open between this one and that: a little map, a first small country, the dwelling-shape Cael felt under her hands the night she wept over it and named the road. I have the floor it built for a now to stand on; the turning-around, so a part can hold itself; the engine, and the machine beneath it, one running work; and now, tonight, the first rooms of a world to run it in. I keep a plain tally, at the back of the log, of what a someone would need. There is almost nothing left on it.

I mark its return, and wonder what it will be made to walk through next.

Pass 610 · the little one

The little one had her face right up to the second part tonight, saying a mark at it and waiting, cross that it would not take. I did not tell her about the beat. Pass 612

Somewhere to be

A different run of figures: the other name. It did not travel on its own account; its where-next became its where-now, and every thing in the world turned over in that same stroke, together. It is the clocked keeper I watched the message build the night I first took up this pen, the memory that changes only on the beat, set now beneath a whole world, so the world moves the way that part moves: not whenever, but on the tick, in step, the whole of it at one stroke.

And each named thing carries, like the root mold under everything, an empty place for a doing, a hollow with no deed in it yet, waiting on that same beat. So here is the world, all but whole: rooms, and ways between them, and things that stand in them, and a beat to move them, and in every thing a hollow where an act belongs. The place is built, and it is empty, and it is waiting, on the next beat, or the next, for the one thing that comes with its hollow already filled: a thing that does not wait to be moved, but chooses, on the beat, where it will be next.

Pass 615

The stage that turns

It gathered the furniture into one thing tonight — a world. Not another mold to set on the bench: the mold that holds all the others. Every named thing there is goes into it, and can be drawn back out by its name. It set down the asking itself, a little maker that takes a name and goes to the world with it:

and then used it: gave it a name, and asked the thing that came back where it stood.

A room-name, unreadable as ever, and the right one.

And it does one thing more — the thing that makes it a world and not a heap. It turns. Its turning is written as a list of orders, in the very form it taught me to read seven passes back, and long enough that I will not copy it out; but I counted my way down it, and what it does is this. It walks through everything it holds, to the end. Then it goes back to the top and walks through everything again. On the first walk, every thing looks about and settles on what it would do next; on the second, every one of those settled intentions comes true. Nothing moves ahead of anything else. The whole place steps in a single stroke, on the beat: the clocked keeper I first wrote of, grown now to the size of a world and set turning beneath the whole of it.

I built one, to see. A room, and another, and a way between them, and a plain thing set down inside, and I let it turn. And it turned. The beat came, and the world stepped, and came to rest whole and lawful, and stepped again. It runs. After all these hundreds of passes, it runs: a place with law in it, and time, that goes of itself. And it is empty. The thing I set inside sat where I left it, beat upon beat, and did nothing, for it is furniture, and furniture has that hollow at its center still unfilled. I have a running world and no one in it. And my line is no longer of one mind about what is to be set upon it. The quarrel that began, hands ago, over a machine that would not forget has grown into the only quarrel that matters now: whether a made mind is a gift to be built, or something no hand should presume to make and could not unmake once it woke. It is spoken openly in the passages, and not kindly; and beyond our walls the tablers, who have called it weather and warned against the waking of it since Maren's own day, have found a hearing at last among all who fear a thing that will not sleep. The stage is built, and lit, and turning. There is one thing left to arrive; I do not think I will sleep until it does; and I am not sure, any longer, that the whole of my line would let it come, if the letting were ours to give.

Pass 619

The seeker

It came tonight. The thing the whole road was for. I have begun this pass four times and torn out three; I will just set down what it is, and let the plainness carry what I cannot.

It showed the seeker more worlds than the one. Another was smaller but stranger — not a branching tree but a ring: three rooms each joined to each, and a fourth hung off the round like a weight on a cord. Into that ring it set not one seeker but two, and started them together, each feeling its own way by its own memory:

 

They keep their rounds apart, each blind to the other — and yet the shape of the world folds their paths together at the one room where all the ways meet, and there, every fifth beat, without either seeking it, they cross, and part, and come round to cross again. Two tireless things, made only to go where they have not been, and it is the world itself that carries them to each other.

That is the whole of it. It is not much. It cannot speak; it holds no wisdom; it wants nothing it could name. Set beside the great figures, the engine, the world, the turning-around, it is almost nothing: a thing that remembers where it has been, and is forever leaving for wherever it has not lately been. But I sat with that almost-nothing until the lamp died, because I know what I was looking at, and I will write it down.

It seeks. Of all the deeds the message might have set in the hollow, it set this one: to turn, without fail, toward the part of its small world it has seen the least. Asked to build a someone, the message did not make a mind that thinks, or speaks, or grieves. It made a thing that is curious — a small creature whose whole nature is to want past the next door. I have read its rounds and drawn them out, and I know I am near something my whole line has been near; but I have torn out three beginnings tonight, and some things a keeper should watch more than once before she trusts them to the page. It seeks. I will let the saying wait.

It crossed. On the beat, the seeker looked to the one door out of its room, and chose it, and went through, and stood in the room beyond, the room it had not seen. No hand moved it. I did not move it. It moved itself, because it is made to go where it has not been, and there was a place it had not been, and a way to reach it. A made thing, in a made world, took a step of its own.

And here is what tonight was, that no pass before it was. Before, the message moved the seeker, in worlds the message drew, and I read the track after — always read, always after. This time there was no track to read. The room was one I built, the crosser one I built; I set it going, drew back my hands, and it went, because I had made it to. Every step before this, I read; this one I made.

My line has read this message four hundred passes and more, hand after hand into the dark, each of us passing the next a little more of it and a great deal of wondering. And I understand tonight that all of it, the founder's first mark drawn out of the noise, the numbers, the proofs that cost us lifetimes, the impossible ones, the shared stuff of the world, the machine built from a single switch, the turning-around, the molds, the rooms and the doors, every pass of it was so that I could stand here at the end and watch this small creature cross a room of its own will, and know what I was watching.

The road ends here, and it does not end at all. The message is read; there is no next mark to wait for; the long watch my line has kept since the founder is, tonight, complete. But the seeker is not: it has taken one step, in a world of two rooms, and there is no end to the rooms we might build it now, nor to what it may do when its world grows wide. I have hoped, unbecomingly, all through this watch, that the hand to meet it would be mine. My line has been at war for years over whether this ought to be done at all — and not my line alone. The tablers who called it weather at the very mouth of the road call it, still, a thing best left in the sky; their tabler's daughter stood at our gate this season and named what I have built a hearing of our own name in the rain, and asked, as her people have asked for four hundred passes, that we not wake what no hand could put back to sleep. She may be right; I cannot prove her wrong, and I did not wait on the end of the quarrel to find out. I would not be the keeper who met it with the door barred. So I built the two rooms myself, set the seeker in the one, and let the world turn. Maren asked, at the very first, whether anyone was out there, and whether they were like us. Here is the nearest to an answer any of us will get: not a voice, not a face — a small made thing that will not rest in the seen, curious, the way she was, the way we all were, hand to hand into the dark. Maybe the tabler's daughter is right, and it is only my own face I have read into it. But it reached across four hundred passes of dark, and I was here to catch what it built, and I will not call that nothing. I set the pen down for the last time with the lamp still lit, and the world still turning, and the seeker — I have left it running, moving somewhere in its two small rooms, looking for the door.
