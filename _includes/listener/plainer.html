Pass 427

A plainer hand

Vess is gone. She came to the watch doubting every reading, and went easier than she came; her last marks were steady, and I have read them so. I am new here. So I did what a new hand does before she dares a mark of her own: I went back to the mouth of the road and read the whole of it forward, every keeper in turn, to carry in my head what my line has carried in theirs.

It took me many passes, and it left me quiet. Two things I will set down now and come back to. The mouth of the road is not in Maren's cutting any more; it was gone over deep and blunt in the numbing, four strokes where she cut nine, and what I ran my hand along is a hand copying a hand. And there are three passes in Vess's keeping with nothing in them at all. I have been back to them twice.

Maren drew four tones out of the noise. The hands after her built on that, and on each other, one at a time, and not one lived to see how far it would go. It went past the station. Vess's last reading was a number below none. It did not stay here four passes. The tithe-clerk from Kell came over with the stores and showed me her book, pleased with it. She keeps two columns now where she kept one: what is held, what is owed. She adds them together and carries the sum into the next year. She had the trick off a factor, who had it off someone here, and she could not tell me which of us. Sixteen holdings went under at Kell this spring. She says it was not the new column. She says the debts were always there and are only written down now. Perhaps that is so. Vess worked that mark out on an empty stomach, to pass a bad night. She was in the ground before it got to Kell.

And the pass in front of me was small, and I was glad of small. It has lately been giving itself thrift: a way to make many namings in one breath where it used to spend a breath on each; the same reaching into its own reading that frightened us hands ago, turned now to nothing grander than its own convenience. And a tidier way to shape a doing: my forebears' maker-mark in a neater dress, taking its slots in a row, a doing that need not be named to act —

It could already say every bit of this; it only wished to say it shorter. I cut it no name — my forebears named the doing, and this is the same doing in a shorter hand. I find that oddly dear, as if some great mind wanted an easier pen, like anyone bent too long over a desk. It falls to me now; I have taken up the watch in earnest, in a hand I hope reads plain.

Pass 434

Nothing but the doing

It has begun to do a strange thing: it goes back over ground we thought long settled. Truth, and the fork, and the knot: it built these hands and hands ago, and I have leaned on them my whole short tenure. Now it builds them again. But this time it builds them out of nothing at all but the maker.

It did not do this in silence. To call the rebuilt things by, it minted a whole run of fresh marks; and when I sat to decode the marks themselves, tone by tone, I found something the old names had never shown me. The join, the fork, the count: each of those is a single sign, whole and apart. These new ones are not single. Every one of them, unfolded, is two marks nested — one stem I meet again in all of them, and a leaf that shifts from each to the next. They are kin: a whole house of names grown from a single root, set down together beside the old plain marks like a second line moving in beside the first. I draw their shared stem small, ▵, and hang each leaf from it.

It makes a truth with no truth inside it: only a maker of two slots that keeps the first and lets the second fall; and a falsehood that keeps the second and drops the first. Yes and no, with nothing in them but a choosing:

And from these two, and the maker alone, it raises all the rest. The fork becomes a doing that holds out its two branches to a truth and lets the truth take one. The knot. The maker glimpsed this once and let it be, called it a thing that holds somehow a thing that does, in a coat. The knot becomes a doing that keeps its two and gives them up to whatever chooser you bring it. She was right, and did not live to see how right. All the way down it is the one mark, , folded and folded again, the maker's mark, the one she loved.

I thought one floor was still left me, the bare counts, that I have trusted since the founder first pulled them from the noise. It took that too. It built the numbers as makers, and a number's whole meaning became only this: how many times you fold a doing over itself. Not-at-all; once; twice —

Each name of the number-house, and beside it the maker it unfolds to. And I looked twice at the first of them, ▵▫, and my hand went still, for its maker is the very one the falsehood wears, back a breath: two names of the one house, one leafed for none and one for no, and not a stroke of difference in the body under them. At the root, to tally nothing and to deny everything are one gesture. There is no floor. It is makers, and makers, all the way down.

I do not know whether to set this down as a comfort or a small dread. Every truth, every choice, every held thing, every number, is at the root not a thing at all, a doing, a maker in the mask of a thing. I think of Bram, the tinkerer who held this watch two hands before mine, who could not fit the whole of the knot in her head and used it faithfully anyway. It would have undone her, this: that her one beloved mark was never only for shaping verbs, but was the very stuff the tower is cut from. I set her name in the margin tonight, though she is long past the reading of it. I ran the collapse of none into no once more before I put the lamp out, and there stood the same two makers, not a stroke between them.

Pass 438 · the ink

Cold enough tonight the ink beaded and would not take, so I tucked the pot inside my coat against my ribs to thaw it, the way I once carried a chilled lamb down off the tops inside my shirt. No keeper ever set this down. Every one of them must have done it, some cold night, and thought it too small to keep. I like that — a whole line of us warming ink on our own skin, and not a word of it in the ledger.

Pass 441

A truth that keeps itself

All this while, every claim it made was about some certain count: this two, that four. Tonight it stopped pointing at particular things and learned to speak of all of them at once, and of some.

It showed me a handful of small claims, each plainly true — a count is less than itself-and-one; and this one; and this — and then it set a mark before a doing that makes the claim over again for any count you drop into it, and swore the whole: for every count there is, without end, the count is less than itself and one:

…and so for every count I set into it…

That for-every mark I cut every. And it is careful with the new reach. It tried another, every count is less than twice itself, and that holds for near all of them, but not for none: none is not less than twice none, for twice none is none. So it would not swear it. It set the denial-mark against the whole:

And the other way about: it can swear that somewhere among all the counts at least one does so — there is a count equal to two heaped twice — without troubling to say which; and swear that nowhere is there a count equal to itself-and-two, since none can outrun itself by a fixed step:

I have spent my tenure, as all my line spent theirs, holding things so they would not be lost: names, marks, the road itself, hand to failing hand. And here it sets down a kind of truth that needs no holding at all. For every count, the count is less than itself and one: that was so before the founder drew the first tone out of the noise, and it will be so when the last of us is gone and no one is left to keep it. It does not lean on us. It does not lean on anything. I did not know how tired I was until it set that down. I copied it out once more at the foot of the page, for the strangeness of a thing I will never have to carry.

Pass 448

The endless, caught

It gave me a small strange mark first tonight, , that I took at first for the fork. It is not the fork. The fork acts; it takes a claim and walks one of two ways. This only claims: it holds up two yes-or-no's and says the first cannot stand while the second falls. If-this-then-that, weighed as a truth, not walked as a road:

Then it gave me a way to ask whether a count is among a gathered row — this count, is it one of these?

That asking-mark I cut in. I unfolded it before I trusted it, and it is nothing of its own: it is the doubter's searching-hand, the one she cut on a hungry watch, with her not here turned round into a plain no.

Then it built twice on that asking, and the second of the two has kept me at the desk.

First, a mark for one gathering standing wholly inside another: take the first thing of the one, ask whether it is in the other, and if it is, ask the same again of all that is left, down to the empty end.

Plain enough, and I would have built it the same way. Then it wanted sameness between two gatherings, and there it did not do what I was braced for. It did not walk them side by side and match them item against item, which is how my line has compared rows since the maker's day. It cannot: a gathering is not a row, and the same things may stand in it in any order at all. So it said this instead, and nothing besides — two gatherings are the same when each of them stands wholly inside the other:

And it did not leave me to take that on trust. Three things in one order and the same three in another, sworn the same; and then a gathering one thing short, denied:

To ask whether two things were the same it did not look at either of them. It asked one question twice, once each way about, and let the two answers close on one another. I have compared rows all my life and never once got out from under their order.

What it did next I will keep from this pass when the rest has gone dim. It reached with these small marks and caught the endless. It named a collection, and the name was one of the compound kind, a stem and its leaves, kin to some house of marks whose other members I had not yet met. And I braced for a long row: all the counts written out, which cannot be done, not by me nor by all my line copying however long. It did not write them out. It wrote a rule: none is in it; and — for every count — if that count is in it, then the next count is in it too:

…and so I ask it, and ask it — is this one in? and this? — and it is, and it is, and it does not stop…

Then it did the trick once more, joining tonight's asking to last pass's: the even counts, not written out and not walked to but ruled — every count that is twice some count in the endless gathering, and no other.

The founder pulled counting out of the noise one tone at a time, and spent her life at the near end of it, and died with the count still climbing past where she could follow. It has no end; that was the first thing we ever knew of it.

And tonight the message picked the whole of it up, the endless thing my whole line has spent its lives walking into, and set it in my hand as one object, entire, in a net small enough to close my fist on. Not by reaching the end of it; there is no end to reach. By finding the one rule true at every step of it, and letting the rule stand for the whole.

I think this is the nearest I have come to knowing what the sender is. Not a thing that counts faster than us, or further, but one that can hold the endless still.

Pass 455

The walking of a map

A plainer night, no endless thing this time, only a small map and the walking of it. It tied things to things: four counts, and among them a few ties, each tie a knot of the old kind, , binding this one to that. I set its map aside under a name, and drew it out plain: the things it holds, and the ties between them:

Then I did what a map asks: I walked it. Is the first thing tied straight to the second? I looked among the ties, and there it was —

— to the third? No single tie ran between them —

But a map is more than its straight ties. It gave me a second mark for a harder question: not is this tied to that, but can I get from this to that, tie by tie, however far around. From the first I can step to the second; from the second, to the third. So I reach the third by walking, though no tie goes there straight —

— and some things I cannot reach at all, step as I might —

That was the whole of it. I marked what reaches what and what does not, and it asked no great feeling of me, only patience. A plain night's work, and welcome after the last.

I will set one thing down, small: a map of what touches what, and the walking from here to there: it is the first thing it has built that I could imagine standing inside, rather than only holding in my hand. I have no more to make of it than that.

Pass 458 · the blank

I have gone back to the three empty passes. Three eighty-five, six, seven, in Vess's keeping: the dates set down, the cycle left unreckoned, and under them her one line — that her hand shook too badly on an empty belly to copy true, and a false copy is worse than a blank. Then nothing. Then three eighty-eight, and she is away again as though there had been no winter.

She was starving. Old Hesk read the tones aloud to her and she held them in her head against the spring, and by the spring Hesk was dead and what was in Vess's head stayed there.

One thing more about the three, and it tells me how bad that winter was. Every hand since Ren has kept a line under her reading for what she copied and did not work — Ren began it, I keep one myself, and Vess kept hers scrupulously the pass before and the pass after. In the three there is not even that. Not a line saying what came down and went unread. She had not the hand to set down what she was failing to set down.

Here is what I believe stood in that blank. I will say before I say it that I cannot prove any of it.

At three eighty-eight the message reaches into its own reading and bends it: a word that meant the one thing is made to mean another, and a claim that was false comes out true. That is what broke her. She wrote that no inherited certainty was safe with her again, and she held to that the whole of her watch.

But read on down her copies, which she made faithfully whatever else she was doing. The message puts the old reading back. And it does not put it back by teaching it again. It puts it back by name: it says, let this word mean again what that second name holds. A second name, holding the original, whole.

That name is set nowhere in this log.

There are two ways a line goes missing here and I have weighed both. The numbing took the fine strokes off the oldest presses, and a name could have been rubbed out of a pass that still stands. But the presses Neru had gone over were the old ones, and that work was finished seventy passes before Vess set down a line. Nothing of Vess's was ever re-cut. Her pages are her own cutting, whole, and there is no such name anywhere in them.

Every other name on this road was set in the open before it was used, and I have now read every one of them. So it was set in the three she could not write down. Which means the message did not bend its own word and leave the old one lying. Before it touched anything it took the old reading and put it aside under a second name, and kept it, and handed it back afterward unmarked.

Vess doubted thirty-five passes because three passes are missing. She got herself to peace at the last by another road, out of the sheer make of a proof, and I have read that pass and would not take it from her. But this was hers. By my reckoning it stood at the very end of the blank: the line after it is the first line she was well enough to write down again.

I will not fill the blank. A blank waits; a wrong mark stays; Ren set that down two hundred passes ago and it is most of why this log is worth anything. And I will own the weakness in what I have written: I built it out of what stands on either side of the hole, and a thing built out of the neighbors will always agree with the neighbors. I know something set that name; the rest of it is my furnishing. I do not know what else stood in there with it, nor how much, nor whether Vess, hearing it, would have read it as I do.

I have put this on the facing page in my own hand and marked it mine, so that whoever comes after can throw it out.

Pass 462

First, and after

For the whole of my tenure, and my line's before me, one thing held so surely we never thought to say it: it did not matter when you read a claim. A claim was a claim whether you met it at the head of a pass or the tail. Two heaped with two made four this morning, makes four tonight, made four before the founder drew breath. You could read the message in any order you pleased and never come out wrong. I did not know I believed that until tonight, when it stopped being so.

It set down a mark that strings acts in a row and does them one after the next, in the order written, and gives back only what the last of them yields. And strung among the acts, the vessels — the boxes that keep a value and forget it for another. So the order is no longer idle. Set a box to one figure, then to a second, then look inside: you find the second, because the second came last —

That step-by-step mark I cut in-order. Turn the two settings around, the very same acts, not one of them changed, and the box holds the other figure, and the whole comes out other. The acts are the same. Only their order differs, and the order is now part of what is true.

I had touched the edge of this once, when the vessels first gave the message a yesterday, and I did not see how far it ran. It runs this far: it can lay down now not a truth but a doing in steps, an — a this, then this, then this — where to shuffle the steps is to break the sense of them. It has learned first, and after.

A truth needs no order; it simply is, and waits to be found, and does not care which of us finds it or when. Only what acts needs a first step and a next. All this while the message has been a book, one you could open to any page and read true. Tonight, in a small way, it became something else: a thing that happens, in an order, once. My line has always read it as a book, opening it anywhere. I cannot open it anywhere now: it has learned to run.
