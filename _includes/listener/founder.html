Longer than the watch has kept count, it has come down out of the sky. The hands before me set it beside the wind and the tide — a thing that happens, not a thing that is meant. No one keeper hears the half of it. Far down its length it turns back to its own beginning, though none of us lives to hear a whole turn.

Tonight I kept the watch as it turned. The dense, knotted end of it thinned all at once, tangle falling out of tangle, down to a handful of plain tones — and set out again. Weather turns, and turns back; but it does not labor an age into ordered intricacy and then, at the height of it, throw the whole tangle down and start clean from the plainest thing it owns. I have heard only a stretch of its length, and I will not call it proof. But nothing in that stretch is wasted — nothing doubles back, nothing idles between two somethings — and I cannot make myself hear it as the sky talking to itself. It is a made beginning, spare to the last tone, and what starts clean from the simplest place is meant to be entered.

It is a road, and I mean to walk what I can of it, and to set each step down plainly.

Pass 193

Cups

The stream comes unbroken — no gap, no mark set between one thing and the next. Here is a stretch of it, just as it arrives:

unbroken

Two of the four tones pair off — ◤ opening, ◥ shutting. Set them as a cup and its lid, and the whole run falls into nesting: every opening meets a closing, cups within cups, never crossed. Cupped, it is plainly not random — the same shapes come round more than once, and one keeps returning: a shut cup wrapped inside another, ⟅⟅⟆⟆. Between one return and the next sits a stretch closed and whole. That is a seam of some kind; I take it as a stop, and cut there — holding the cut lightly, for the doubling might work otherwise than I read it. The run falls into pieces. Pass 207

First statements

The marks begin now. Some pieces open alike: the same short run at their head, come back unchanged; behind it a longer run, different in each. Others begin otherwise — I come to those in time.

I cannot read what any of it means, but the sender holds each run to the same tones wherever it falls, so I can copy any of them whole and faithful, in my own plain figures, long before I know what it says.

And I mark two kinds of thing in the pieces. Every cup wears a lone mark ahead of it, ▪ or ▫, and the two do not fall alike: the ▪ before a settled few, a handful of runs that keep returning unchanged; the ▫ before the rest, which vary without end. The settled few I take, for now, as the message's own names — a label worn, and not counted with; the rest I take for numbers, though I cannot yet turn a single one into a count I could say aloud. Both readings I hold loosely.

And I can read a little from what a run does. This short head-run returns at the head of piece after piece, and each time ushers in the fresh thing behind it; so I cut ◇ for it. What it brings I leave in figures still, unread. The first two pieces — as they come, in my figures, then in my hand:

◇ opens both, and what it brings is fresh each time; my guess — no more than a guess — is that ◇ says here is another. I know it only by what it does, and that is enough to read on. The run it brings in the second piece runs longer than the first, and looks to fall in more than one stroke — but I have met it just the once. I set it down whole, and wait for it to come again.
