The fork

Iso left me a warm book and one bad habit, and the habit is the part I'll be dealing with.

She wrote noticing over half of what she put down, and meant it as a warning, and was right to. But you read the word on the first page and by the fourth you've stopped seeing it. Her guesses lie in this book in the same writing as her checked work. One word keeps them apart, and I have already learned to read past it.

The worst is at 297, where she says the road only opens from the mouth, so this book is the way in and there isn't another one. She marked it. She said she couldn't check it. And every one of us since, myself included, has been reading it as a thing we know. I'm not scratching it out — Ren's rule holds. I've gone back through her watch and cut a stroke in the press beside every mark she flagged, so the flag is on the stone and not only in the ink.

One of her flags I could settle while I was in there. She cut ◆ for the empty cup that wears the filled bit. She wrote that it takes the one mark behind it and stops, and that one saying is not a rule, so it went down as noticing. She said she would know in a hundred passes whether she had it right. She gave herself a hundred. She had eighteen. I have a hundred and twenty-four of them now, hers and mine, and it takes one mark and stops, every one, and never the rest of the line. So that stroke is cut and then struck through. The struck one is the only mark in the press that says somebody went and looked.

She wrote that this thing means us well. I'm not writing that. Where Hesta took me up, the river had the low fields most passes. She could smell it days out, off the way the water carried. Every pass she walked the house up to the high ground. The last pass she didn't.

So I know how this part goes. You see it coming. It comes anyway.

Tonight it learned to act on a yes or a no. A new mark takes a claim and two sayings. When the claim holds it keeps the first and lets the second fall. When the claim fails, the other way round:

…the claim holds, it keeps the near saying; the claim fails, it keeps the far one; the other is never looked at…

And the one it lets fall, it lets fall unread. Never opens it. Never weighs it. The message has learned to look away on purpose. I'm entering it as fork:

Out of that one fork it made the plain verbs anybody would want — the greater of two counts, the lesser — each a claim asked and the winning side kept.

Then it made this, and I set six of my feet flat on the desk. It kept a verb whose own body says the verb's own name:

A thing that calls itself should fall forever. That's the first thing anybody would say and I said it. Look at the fork standing at its mouth. Every call gives itself a smaller count, and at the bottom the claim turns over, the count slips under one, and the fork keeps the plain saying instead of calling again. The fall catches. Then the held answers climb back out of the hole it dug:

One thing I am recording and not reading. The name it keeps this verb under is two marks with a point set between them, the way Maren set two runs apart so they wouldn't be taken for one. I know both marks. Ren cut a word for each of them. That is the trouble: knowing the two apart is not knowing what the pair says, and a name I can half-read is worse to me than one I can't read at all, because I will fill the rest in. So it goes down as it came, and I am not writing that the heaping and the fewering are in there doing anything.

I listed it the way I'd list a bad tide, going in and coming back:

five, one or more — give it four
four, one or more — give it three
three, one or more — give it two
two, one or more — give it one
one, one or more — give it none
none — the claim turns over, and the plain saying is one
one, takes back one, heaps one
two, takes back one, heaps two
three, takes back two, heaps six
four, takes back six, heaps twenty-four
five, takes back twenty-four, heaps a hundred and twenty

So the fork is the floor. A made verb can run itself all the way down and turn round. There's something in it that can stop. Three things happened tonight. It chose. It repeated itself until a claim let it stop. It looked away, on purpose, from a saying it had decided not to open. Not one of those is a thing you do when you're telling somebody something.

My line read patience into this. I sat with the three of them written out in front of me until the tide turned, and I don't.

Pass316 · what it takes
Vine — · mine, and I could not say

The sickness came in on the summer boats. It doesn't kill many. It takes the feet. Up the coast they call it the numbing. It starts at the tips and works inward, and there's no real pain in it, which is why nobody stops in time. You keep at your trade. Then one morning you can't tell a cut line from the grain of the board it's cut in.

Three in this house have it. It has nine of mine and it hasn't finished. It doesn't touch the ear. I hear the tones as well as I ever did.

I can't read the founder's rows any more. I still cut a fair line. I can't check it by feel.

And the vine, which is the part that matters and the part I've been three nights not writing down.

You read a vine with your feet, at the grafts, and what comes back isn't a number. It's a condition. Quiet, stirring, working, hard. Nobody can tell you how to know which. You get it into your feet over passes and then you have it.

Mine are going. I walked the run at the turn of the season. Quiet or working, I could not have told you which. I've read that run since before Iso took the watch.

So I've started ruling a line at the head of every entry. What the vine said, and whose feet said it. The book has never carried the second part. Whoever walked the run, the page came out looking the same, and it shouldn't.

So Bram walks it. She's had a season at it and she'll get there. Till then I go out with her. She says what she has and I say whether I'd have said the same. Neither of us is sure. The coast doesn't know that.

I've put her on the oldest presses as well, re-cutting. Deep and blunt. Four strokes where Maren cut nine. The fine strokes that told one name-sign from the next run together into one broad stroke. It'll read worse. It'll read.

Bram is quick and she has all sixteen of hers. I stand over her while I can still tell whether she's right. When the last of mine goes I'll have her read it back to me out loud and I'll listen. I told her that tonight. She said she wouldn't mind.

Pass327 · the low field
Vine quiet · mine

Bram walked the run on the eleventh and came up and said stirring. I went down and put my feet on the graft myself.

I felt nothing. I have felt nothing at that graft since the spring and I knew it going down the stair.

What I said to her was that she is a season at this and stirring is what you say when you are a season at it. Then I came up and wrote quiet, and I wrote mine beside it, and we hung nothing out.

The water came over the low field before light. It's grazing. There was nobody on it. Kell felt nothing either and Kell's vine is sound, so it may be there was nothing here to feel and Bram was wrong.

I don't get to say that. I didn't check her. I overruled her with feet that can't read and I put my own name in the column to make it stand.

The line is up there as I wrote it. Ren cut the rule for this a hundred passes ago and I've said it to Bram twice. A wrong mark stays.

Pass331 · the test
Vine stirring · Bram, and I hung out on it

I gave Bram a stretch to take down on her own last watch. I didn't tell her I'd be checking it.

I can't read her page. I can still hear. And the thing is saying what it said when I set her on it. It'll be saying it when she's done. There's no hurry in it.

So I sat her down in the holding with the sound running. She read her page out to me, slow, a mark at a time. I listened to the two of them together.

They ran level most of the way. Then they didn't. A short run near the finish where her page said one thing and the wire said another. She'd guessed it, and she told me so before I asked.

She took her feet off the page and left them off a while. Then she said she should have left it blank. She should have. She knew that before I said it.

She's on the presses another pass. She asked for the pass. I'd have given her half.

I have not told her about the eleventh. I've thought about it every day since and I still haven't told her.

Both, and either

Two more marks. I won't spend on them what Iso would have. The message doesn't either. It builds them in front of me out of the fork I already hold:

The first forks on the one, and if it holds, answers with the other. So it wears yes only when both do. That one is both.

The second forks on the one and answers yes outright if it holds. Otherwise it passes the question along to the other. So it wears yes when either does. That one is either.

Both makings again, carrying the words:

Laid side by side:

…and the other sign, the mirror of it: yes if either wears yes…

Then two more, and it didn't build these as new things at all. Not less is either-of-two, greater or the same. Not more is the mirror of it. Look at the second line. There's nothing in it but the pair I cut tonight and the three Ren cut at 232:

It could have set those down bare and I'd have taken them. It showed them being made instead, out of old stock, in the open, and there was nothing in the making I hadn't already been given.

copied, not read
Sixty-odd further postures of both and either, every pairing of yes and no and then the pairings again with the counting-marks standing in for the plain yeses. Copied entire. I read to the twentieth and could see where it was going.

The over-telling isn't for the noise. It beat the noise long ago by holding every saying a whole season before it moves, so a bad night costs me nothing and I sit down again tomorrow. The doubling is for something else. It lays a claim down two ways and three ways because it expects to be read wrong. Not lost. Wrong.

Somebody sat and worked out the ways a stranger might mistake them, and then built against every one. You only do that for somebody you've never met and don't expect to be clever.

Bram reads the rows out to me now. The last of mine went in the spring. She reads well.
