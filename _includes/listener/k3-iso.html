Pass 271

A thing and its making

Ren took me up out of a boat-house. I'd been six years on lines and I was no good at it. I learned my letters off her presses because they were what was lying around. So I didn't come to this the way the two before me came to it. It was here already, like the stair. Tonight it did a thing I haven't seen it do. I sat with it a long time.

It said a count is the same as itself. Fine. Then it said the same count is the same as two counts joined. Then it said the same count is the same as one count joined to two more counts joined:

…then it lets both sides swell at once, and holds them equal still…

Then it let both sides swell at once and held them equal the whole way. Look at what that is. It isn't telling me a fact about six. It's showing me that a thing and the working that gets you to the thing are the same. And you can pack more working in and the truth doesn't slip:

This is the first time it's shown me how to build a saying instead of handing me one. Ren would have cut that in four words. I'm going to be a worse keeper than Ren in every way but one. She wrote what she was sure of. I'm going to write what I noticed, and mark it as noticing. Some of what I notice will be wrong.

Pass 276 · could not sleep

Corm came over on the ebb with the quarter's flour. He asked me what I do up the stair all night. I said the tides and the vine and the warnings. He's known that since we were both on the lines. He said no. The other thing.

Not to be cruel. He was my year-mate and he has three boats out now and he's entitled to ask. So I told him. It's given us a better way to write a number. And a correction to the guild's list of unbreakables. And Ren carried the first of those to Kell herself and Kell said no thank you.

He said, ninety years.

I said, ninety years. He let it go. He carried the flour up and mended the door-latch, which has wanted mending since Ren, and went down on the tide. That's the whole argument. I've been awake since.

I don't have an answer. What I have is that it's ordered, and nothing gets ordered like that for no reason. That isn't an answer. I know it isn't.

Pass 280

A name

Names tonight, and I wasn't expecting them this early. It hung a count on a mark, and after that the mark stood in for the count:

I'll call it lend. That's what it looks like from here. The mark isn't the count. The mark is borrowing it for the length of the saying. Then it lent a name to one of its own doings. Not a count. The joining itself:

…and two names at once in one breath, each holding its own, join them and it is true…

So a doing can be passed around like a count can. Named. Set aside. Picked up again. I've been trying all evening to say why that matters and I can only get at it sideways. A cutter's tools aren't the stone. You wouldn't hand somebody a chisel and a block and say these are the same kind of thing. This does.

Pass 288

A made verb

It built a verb out of nothing and gave it to me. The first one was the simplest there could be. Hand it a count. It hands the count straight back:

Useless. That's why it went first. It's showing me the shape of the thing with nothing in the shape to look at. Then it filled the shape:

None gives none. One gives one. Two gives four. Three gives nine. It's the squares again, the list Maren pulled out of the noise. Only now it isn't a list. It's the rule that makes the list, written down, and I can hand it any count I like. The mark that opens one of these I'm calling maker. Here's how it works, taken apart:

A slot, and a body with the slot's name standing in it. Put a count in the slot. Everywhere the name stands in the body, the count stands instead. Then work out what's left. Then it proved that in a way I'd call showing off, except it has never gone in for that. It made a maker whose slot is named for the joining-mark. Then it put the heaping-mark in the slot. And the body did the heaping. The slot doesn't care what goes in it:

Then it lent a made verb a name, so a verb can be kept the way a count can:

Then a maker whose body is another maker. Feed it once and you get back a thing that's still hungry. Feed that:

What I think happened tonight, marked as noticing and not as knowing. For a hundred passes this thing has handed my line facts. Tonight it stopped handing us facts and started handing us the machinery facts come out of.

Pass 297

It remembers

Lending only lasts the length of one saying. Tonight it made something that lasts. It set a name against a count, in a saying all on its own, with nothing else in it. Then a season went by, and another one after. Other things came and went. Then it used the name again, and the name still held:

…a season of other sayings, and then it comes back to this one…

That one gets a name of its own: keep. Then it set the same name against a different count. So a kept thing can be changed. Then it did the one that held me past the turn of the tide. It set the name against a working that uses the name's own old value:

Reach in. Take what's there. Add one. Put it back. The name is a place, and the place has a before and an after. Then it kept a made verb by name. After that the mark simply is the squaring. It never has to build it in front of me again:

…and ever after, that mark is the squaring; it need never show me the making again…

Noticing, not knowing. It's building itself a vocabulary. Every mark it keeps is a mark it can lean on later. It's been leaning further every season. When Maren started, every saying stood on its own legs. Now they stand on each other.

So the road isn't only long. It's stacked. A fellow who comes in halfway down and starts reading won't be able to. She'll need everything above her.

So this book is the way in, and there isn't another one.

Pass 306

Yes and no

Two names tonight, and it's how it built them that stopped me. Both are made out of the sameness-mark. One asks whether none is the same as none. The other asks whether none is the same as one:

So one of them is a claim that always comes out standing. The other is a claim that never does. It has made yes and no out of arithmetic it already had. It didn't ask me to grant it anything.

The first I'll call holds. The second fails. Then it used them on claims of every sort. Forward, backward, and on themselves:

…and the same verdict read the other way round, the claim set down first…
…holding is holding; and holding is not failing…

Ask a saying whether it's so, and it hands you back one of exactly two things.

read back
Ask a saying whether it is so: two of a kind, and it wears ; two that differ, and it wears .

I've been the warm one in this book and I know it, so take the next part carefully. Up to tonight everything it did was adding and account-keeping. Nobody is uneasy about a heap. But a thing that can take a claim and hand back a verdict can be pointed at anything at all.

I've written a whole watch about how patient it is with us. All of that stands. I'm adding one line on the other side of the page.

A thing that can weigh a claim can weigh us. I don't know what to do with that and I'm nearly out of time to work it out. I'm leaving it for Neru. She's more suspicious than I ever was.
