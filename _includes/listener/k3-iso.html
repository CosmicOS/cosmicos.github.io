A thing and its making

Tonight it did a thing I haven't seen it do, and I sat with it until the tide turned. I learned my letters off Ren's presses because they were what was lying around, so I didn't come to this the way the two before me came to it. It was here already, like the stair.

It said a count is the same as itself. Fine. Then it said the same count is the same as two counts joined. Then it said the same count is the same as one count joined to two more counts joined:

…then it lets both sides swell at once, and holds them equal still…

It isn't telling me a fact about six. It's showing me that a thing and the working that gets you there are the same, and you can pack more working in and the truth doesn't slip:

It also closes something Ren left open. At 246 she let join reach a count standing outside its cup, and filed that as a habit and not a rule, because she had seen it once. Here it is four times over, and one of them is a joining shut inside another joining. A cup with a mark in it stands where a plain count stands. She was right.

This is the first time it's shown me how to build a saying instead of giving me one. Ren would have cut that in four words. I'm going to be a worse keeper than Ren in every way but one. She wrote what she was sure of. I'm going to write what I noticed, and mark it as noticing. Some of what I notice will be wrong.

Which means I need somewhere to put it when I find out. So I'm ruling the outer half of every page and leaving it empty. If I'm wrong I'd rather write it beside the wrong thing than at the back where nobody goes.

It remembers

Lending only lasts the length of one saying. Tonight it made something that lasts. It set a name against a count, in a saying all on its own, with nothing else in it. Then a season went by, and another one after. Other things came and went. Then it used the name again, and the name still held:

…a season of other sayings, and then it comes back to this one…

That one gets a name of its own: keep. The setting-down itself, over again:

Then it set the same name against a different count. So a kept thing can be changed. Then it did the one that held me past the turn of the tide. It set the name against a working that uses what the name is already holding:

Reach in. Take what's there. Add one. Put it back. The name is a place, and the place has a before and an after. Then it kept a made verb by name, and after that the mark simply is the squaring:

…and ever after, that mark is the squaring; it need never show me the making again…

Noticing, not knowing. It's building itself a vocabulary, and every mark it keeps is one it can lean on later. When Maren started, every saying stood on its own feet. Now they stand on each other.

Noticing, and I want this one marked hardest of all, because it's the one I most want to be true. If the road only opens from the mouth, then this book is the way in and there isn't another one, and every night any of us has spent up here was the only way anybody was ever going to get at it.

There's no way to check that from inside the book. I'm putting it down because it's what I think and I'd rather it stood where somebody can knock it over.

Yes and no

Two names tonight, and it's how it built them that stopped me. Both are made out of the sameness-mark. One asks whether none is the same as none. The other asks whether none is the same as one:

One is a claim that always comes out standing, the other one that never does. It has made yes and no out of arithmetic it already had, and didn't ask me to grant it anything.

And it numbered them one and none. The mark it chose for yes is the one-mark, and the mark for no is the none-mark, and those are the two figures every count in this book is written with. I don't know whether that is meant. It goes in the ruled half.
