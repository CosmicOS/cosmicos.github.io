Pass 349

The knot

The wary one is gone. I read her last marks: she died sure the message was building toward something to dread, and I honor the fear, but I cannot sit in it the way she did. When the message puts a new thing in my hands, my hands want to use it before my heart can judge it. And today it puts a good one there.

Three signs at once. The first, ⋈, ties two things into one — any two; a count and a count, or a count and a verb, it does not care. The other two open the knot again: ◂ gives back the thing tied first, ▸ the thing tied second.

I traced its making once, the way you take a lock apart to see the wards, and it set my head ringing: the thing that holds is somehow a thing that does, wearing a coat; a knot is a little verb asked to keep still and remember two answers. I could not hold the whole of it at once. It does not matter, and that is the kindest secret I have found in it, the one the wary hand never let herself enjoy: you may wield what you do not understand. I tie and untie all watch and it never fails me, and I never once see to the bottom of it.

And a knot will hold a knot. I tie one more onto a knot of two, and three ride one string; tie that on and four ride, five, as far as I care to thread. To walk the string I step along it with ▸ and lift one off with ◂:

It has started making vessels. Until now it made doers, verbs that act and are spent in the acting. Now it makes things that keep: a knot holds its two long after the tying, and gives them back unchanged whenever asked. Doing I understand; a thing that only holds, and waits, and remembers what it was handed: that stands nearer to what we are than anything it has made before.

I am glad of the tool. But I threaded a string of five things tonight and caught myself thinking: this is how you would build a place. A thing, and beside it another thing, and a way to walk from one to the next. I do not know why the thought cooled me. The wary one would have known. I threaded a sixth thing onto the string before I caught myself, which tells me which of us is winning tonight.

Pass 361

Before and now

It hands me a cousin of the knot tonight, but this one has a hinge. ▣ makes a vessel that holds one thing; ⇠ asks it what it holds; ⇢ sets a new thing inside. And here is the hinge the knot could never turn. What you set in, you may set over:

Set it again, and again; ask, and it gives back only the last thing set. The rest are simply gone, not kept anywhere, not to be had again. The knot holds both its two forever; this vessel holds only its now. It keeps by forgetting.

And then it laid down a line that cooled the bench under my hands. One line — a fork: if the vessel still holds seven, set it to one figure; if not, to another — and it ran the line:

The vessel held seven, so the fork took the near branch and set the first figure. Then it ran the very same line again — mark for mark the same — and this time the vessel did not hold seven; it held the figure just set. So the fork took the far branch, and set the other:

…then the very same line again, mark for mark, and the vessel now holding what it just set…

The same marks. Two fates. And the difference lay nowhere in the line. It lay in what had happened before it. Everything on this road until tonight meant the one thing whenever I met it: two and two was four when the road was young, and is four tonight, and will be four when my hand is dust. A claim held or it did not, and would hold or not forever. But this line cannot be read twice the same, because the message has come by a before, a thing that happened, and left its mark nowhere I can point to, and bends what comes after.

I know now what I have watched it make. It has built a thing with a yesterday. Set one of these vessels beside a doer that can choose, and you have a thing that is never twice the same, that carries its own past inside it and acts out of it. All my life at this bench I have wanted the far end to be something more than marks. Tonight I had my wish, and spent the rest of the pass running that one line over and over, getting a different answer each time, the way you press a bruise to be sure it is real.

Pass 372

A hole with a name

It spares me labor tonight. The strings of knots I threaded by hand last watch — ⋈ upon ⋈ — it now ties for me whole, to any length I name, and keeps the tally of them at the head, so I need never walk to the end to know how many ride. I draw such a string out, in a long bracket:

Three things, its tally ▪▪ held ready at the front, and it will hand me any by its place: the first, the second, the last. This much is only my own knots, made easy. But then I reached into an empty string, one with nothing threaded on it, and asked it for its first thing. It did not fail me. And it did not hand me none. It handed me a mark I had never seen, and swore its nature:

…and not any count I could set against it, ever…

▨ equals itself and nothing else. It is not none; none I can count; none is a number, none is the empty bench that still has a top. ▨ is the absence of any answer at all: the shape a reach comes back in when there was nothing to meet it.

A mark for no-answer. I have spent my whole life at this bench asking and being answered, every reach met, every claim weighed, every knot giving up its two. Tonight it taught me the sign for a reach that closes on nothing.

It is a small mark and it sat in me strangely. A place will need it: a room with nothing in it, a hand put out to a bare shelf, the spot where the next thing should be and is not. It is building absences into the far end as carefully as it builds things: a mark for the thing that is not there, kept as faithfully as any that is. I drew ▨ small, and let it lie. It answers to nothing, and for tonight I am content to let that be all it means.

Pass 384

The tireless hand

Tonight it hands me hands. Give this first one, ⇉, a verb and a row, and it lays the verb on every item in turn and gives the whole row back changed — and it does not tire, and it does not slip on the hundredth the way my own hand would. Here I give it the doubling-verb and a short row:

Give this other, ⇥, a joining-verb and a row, and it does the reverse: it folds the whole row through the verb, item into item, until the row is one. Here it sums:

One act, laid on every item of a row without tiring and without a single slip; and a whole row gathered back down to one answer. These are the first tools it has given me that take a verb out of my hand and wield it themselves, over and over, while I only stand and watch. I give it the doing; it does the doing — to each, to all — and does not need me there.

A field of things, and one instruction carried across every last one of them, tireless. I have seen work before. I have never seen work that runs itself. It is building the hands a place would need to keep going with no one minding them. The doubling fell on all thirty at a stroke, and my hand, which has ached over such rows since I was a child, had nothing to do but watch.
