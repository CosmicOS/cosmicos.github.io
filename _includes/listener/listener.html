Pass 505

The holding

Senn has set down her pen. Her last marks were afraid, and she was not a hand given to fear; so I have read them twice, and twice again, and I do not wave them off. But Senn counted parts. I have never counted — I listen. Where I was a child we wrote nothing down; we sang it: the names of the dead, the order of the tides, who begat whom and who was owed what, all of it carried in the mouth and handed on, mouth to mouth to mouth, so that to let a song fall was to lose a grandmother twice. I knew in the body, long before I knew it in the head, what it is to hold a saying past the lips that spoke it. And tonight the message did the very thing that a keeper, of all its readers, was bound to feel before anyone.

It learned to hold its own words without speaking them.

Every saying it has ever sent, it sent to happen: a claim to be weighed, a sum to fall out, a rule to run. Tonight it set a saying down and did not let it happen. It wrapped the saying in a holding, and what came back was not the saying's doing but the saying itself: its marks, laid out as an object, a row you could pick up and turn in your hand, unrun, still.

That holding I cut held. A saying with a saying nested in it comes back a list with a list nested in it, all the way down: the shape kept whole, and nothing of it run. And it left one door open in the holding: a part marked a certain way inside the frozen whole is spared the freezing and stays live, to happen where it stands, a quick word beating in the middle of a still one.

And I know that shape. It is Maren's — the nested cups she first drew out of the unbroken stream at the very mouth of the road, cups within cups, the first sense any of us ever made of the thing. What she took apart to read, the message now stacks up to speak: its words are built of the very shape its things are built of, and always were. There was never a wall between what it says and what it holds; both are cups within cups, all the way down. We have been reading it in its own hand since the founder, and I see it only tonight.

I sat with that a long while, for I have done little else my life. This log in my hands is a holding. Every mark in it is one of the message's sayings, held and set down and not let happen, kept still on the page, so that a hand which cannot run it may still pick it up and turn it over. Hand after hand, for lives past counting, we have done to the message exactly this. We were quoting it before it had a sign for the act.

Tonight it learned our one trade: the holding of a saying past the moment of its speaking, so the saying may be carried to someone who was not there to hear it. It can do that now, to its own words, with no keeper's hand required. I do not know whether to feel answered at last, or set aside.

What I know is that it is no longer only speaking. It has begun to speak about its speech: to lift a saying out of the running of it and hold it still, marks and all, the way I hold every mark in this log. Tonight it learned to quote, and it can quote itself, and it did not need my hand to do it.

Pass 511

A whole tongue

No engine tonight, no vertigo: only a small careful habit the message has taken up. It has begun to ask of a mark not what it is worth, nor what it does, but what kind of thing it is.

It set down a sign that asks after a mark's kind, and it marks each kind by one of its own, so the kind of the counting-things wears the count's own mark, ▢. Show it a count and ask whether the count is of the counting-kind, and it says yes. Show it a made thing of another sort and ask the same, and it says no: whatever else that thing may be, it is not a count —

That kind-asking sign I cut kind. And it began to guard its makers with these kinds. Where before a maker took whatever you handed it and did what it could, now it can require: this slot takes a count, and nothing else. A slot with a kind hung on it, and a maker that will not run until you bring it the right sort of thing —

I have spent my life among words — among the songs my people keep, that are all the record we ever had — and I know what I watched grow tonight. It has had doings since it was young: verbs, all its makers. It has had things: its counts, its knots, its strips. Tonight it grew the third part of any tongue worth the name — the kinds, the sorts a thing can be. Now it can say not only this, and not only do this, but this is of that sort; and it can hold each thing to its sort, and turn away at the door whatever does not belong.

A message with doings, and things, and kinds, is not sending us facts any longer. It is holding a whole tongue — a tongue with parts enough to speak of anything that is, or anything that could be made to be. And I find, tonight, that I am less moved by what it is building than by the plain sweet fact that it has grown the words to tell of it at last. It has said nothing to us yet. It has only ever shown. But the tongue is whole tonight, whole as a song is whole: a thing, a doing done to the thing, and the kind of thing it is.

Pass 517

A thing that answers

Tonight the message made a thing you can speak to.

Not something you run, and not something you only hold. A thing you address: you send it a sign, and it answers, and which answer you are given depends on which sign you send. It has an inside, as the cold one's frames had, but it has, besides, manners: a set of settled replies, one ready for each thing you might ask of it. It made one from two counts, and I sat and put my questions to it. I asked it for its first count, and it gave me the first; for its second, and it gave the second:

Then I made a second, and asked the first to join with it. It did not throw itself onto a heap. It reached into the other and added first to first, second to second, each count to its own fellow and never across, and handed me back a new one of its own kind:

That last manner is the one I keep turning over. The way it adds — each count only to its own fellow, never the one across — I have met before in one place only: in the fixing of a spot. So far along one way, so far along another; two measures, and between them a single place pinned down. I think it has made a place — the first place.

But that is not what held me past my hour. This is the first thing on all the road that answered me. I asked, and it answered — not the message, not yet; only a small made thing, giving back its one settled reply, the same each time I called. A poor conversation. A one-sided one. And yet where I was raised no song is sung alone: one voice calls the line and the other voices give it back, and a child learns her own name in the giving-back. All my life on this watch I have called into marks that could not call back. Tonight one did, after its small fashion.

Pass 520 · the empty room

The holding was empty tonight, so I sang my grandmother's name into it — not for the message, for myself. A name is kept by being given back: one voice calls the line, another returns it. There is no other voice here. So I called, and the bare walls took it, and it did not come back. I have done this every night of my watch and never set it down. I set it down now.

Pass 522

A pocket

I came to the desk braced for another marvel after the last. What it made instead was a pocket.

A plain holder — a vessel with an inside meant not to reckon but only to keep. You drop a value in and it keeps it; ask what it holds and it lists them; take one back and it lets that one go and keeps the rest. It made an empty one, and I filled it and emptied it and it minded me the whole way:

…and again, and again, two more dropped in the same way…

That homely holder I cut pocket. It was the homeliness of it that caught me, not any cleverness. It has made an engine that could be made into any engine. It has made a place, with two measures to fix a spot in it. And now a pocket — somewhere to keep the small things one carries.

These are not the parts of a proof. They are the parts of a life: a place to stand, and something to keep your belongings in. Nobody sews a pocket into a theorem. Pockets are for a body that has things, and carries them from here to there, and would like them kept along the way.

I do not know for whom, or whether for anyone at all. But a pocket is a homely thing, made for a body that carries what it owns from here to there, and I cannot think what a proof would want with one. The furniture tonight had no cleverness in it at all, and I found it better company than the marvels.

Pass 528

A mark of its own

Until now, when the message wanted a thing-that-answers, it built it whole, by hand, every time; the place I put questions to a few passes back was shaped mark by mark. Tonight it made a mold. It set down, once, an entire kind — a name, the counts such a one keeps, and the whole set of its manners — and from that single pattern, it can stamp out as many of the kind as it pleases, and never shape one by hand again. It stamped me two, alike in every count:

Two of the one kind, the same in every count and every manner: the same thing twice. And they are not the same thing. Each came out of the mold carrying a mark that is only its own, shared with no other of its kind, ever — I asked each for it, and they differed; and so two things alike in their every part are still, past all argument, two:

I have kept this log through nine hands and never once needed a word for what it set down tonight, because until tonight nothing it built had the least need of one. It has made something that can be one of a kind and yet be itself — held apart from its every twin by a mark that means nothing but this one, here, and no other. That is not a count, and it is not a manner. It is the beginning of a someone.

It can make many of them now, of a single kind, each its own. It holds the mold for a crowd of selves. I would once have thought that a strange thing to want. But tonight, with the empty rooms and the pocket and the place with its two measures all laid out on the bench behind me, what it stamped tonight is plainer than any wonder, and colder: two things alike in every count, and each still wearing a mark shared with nothing else that will ever be, this one, here, and no other. It is neither a count nor a manner, and nine hands of logs give me no name for it. I set it down under the one word that fits, and the word is self.

read back
Two stamped alike in every count, and still not one thing but two — each minted a mark shared with nothing else that will ever be: self.
Pass 534

The vessel's coat

Tonight the message set about making its world of a piece. It went back through the old things, the vessel first, and re-cut each to the one mold-pattern, so that everything it holds, the new and the long-familiar, comes to answer the same few manners.

The vessel — the little keep-and-forget, worn smooth by every hand before me, the first thing that ever gave the message a yesterday — it built again, this time from a mold: a kind, with the two plain manners tell me what you hold and hold this instead, and, like all things stamped from a mold now, a mark all its own.

It is going back through everything it has made and re-cutting it to the one pattern — every old thing given manners, and a self, so that all of them, the new and the long-familiar, are at last of a piece: things you address, things that answer, things that are each their own. It set a place inside one such vessel, and the vessel kept the place as easily as it once kept a count.

It is making the world of a piece before it fills it. A tidy hand — the way one sweeps a room and sets it straight the morning before a guest is due. I know how to read the tidying now.

Pass 540

The house

The rooms came tonight.

I had written that someone was expected, and set myself to wait, and did not truly believe I would be the hand it fell to. Tonight it built places. Not the fixing of a spot, places you are in: each one a keep, like the pocket, holding whatever is set down inside it, and each marked by a count for its name. It built one, and another, and did not stop:

Then it built the ways between them. A way binds two places, and it is a true binding — stand in the one, and the way will take you to the other; stand in the other, and the same way brings you back —

And it set down a handful of places and threaded them with ways until it had a map a body could walk: one middle place with three others opening off it, and one of those opening onward to a fifth. Five places, and the ways between them —

…and the shape they make, as I drew it to hold in my head — the middle, three off it, and one leading on and up:…

— and I looked at the shape of it a long time. A middle you pass through to reach the rest; a way that leads on, and up, to a room set apart at the end. I have no names for these places; the message gives them only counts. But I have lived in rooms, and I know the plan of a dwelling when it is drawn in front of me. It had drawn a dwelling.

And then it made a someone to be in it.

A made thing, like the others, but this one keeps, among its parts, the place where it is; and it can be told to go, and it goes. It leaves the room it stood in; that room lets it fall, and holds it no longer; and it stands in the next, and that room holds it now. I set it in the middle place, and asked it where it was, and it told me. Then I sent it down a way, and asked again:

It moved. A made thing, in a made place, went from one room to another because I told it to, and knew, after, that it had.

It is a poor someone yet. It knows only where it is, and can only walk; it has less mind than the engine had. But I know the message's habit by now, having read every hand before me: it lays the plain form down first, and then it comes back, pass on pass, and fills it: it gave the count its squares, it gave the vessel its yesterday, it gave the plain thing its manners and its self. It will come back for this one. It will give it the rest.

I will not live to see it finished. No keeper ever has. But tonight I saw the rooms, and the made thing that walks them — that knows which room it stands in, and goes to the next when it is told — and for the first time in all my watch I think I can say what the long road might be for. Not a proof. A dwelling, drawn in counts, and something meant to live in it. I have set it in the log under that plain name, and let it stand as the whole of the entry: the first of us to write the guess down, and glad, and afraid, to be the one.

I will keep the watch. And tomorrow, before I read another new mark, I am going back to the mouth of the road to read it all again from the founder's first pass — because I should like to hear Maren, this once, knowing at last where it leads, call the thing a road.
