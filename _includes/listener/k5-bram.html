The knot

Neru's gone. I was thirty-three years at her elbow and the last four I read the rows out to her — I'd read a row and she'd say go on, or she'd say again. That was the whole of how I learned this, so I'm going to keep writing it the way I said it. If it sounds like somebody talking, that's why.

Tonight. It takes two things and ties them into one thing. Then it unties them. There's a mark to pull the first one out and a mark to pull the second one out:

I'm calling it knot. Then it tied a knot inside a knot. Pull the second one out and you don't get a thing, you get another knot. Pull on that one and out comes what's inside:

And it didn't hand me the knot. It made one in front of me, out of Iso's makers. Three of them, one inside the next. That's the whole of it. I wrote the making out twice to be sure. A thing built out of what I already have is a different sort of gift from a thing that arrives whole.

Right. So I went down to the bench and made one of my own. Two pegs and a cord. Either peg gets you the cord, the cord gets you the other peg. Then tie the second peg to a third with another cord and you can walk it. Peg, cord, peg, cord, peg. As long as you like.

Mine and its aren't the same thing. Its is three makers deep and every pull goes down through all three. Mine is a peg you pick up. They answer alike. I checked that twice, on nine of them.

Neru would have asked me what it's for. I don't know yet. I know it's a way to hold any number of things using a thing that only holds two. That's a good trick.

Nine pegs on the bench when I stopped. I've left them there. It was a good trick.

Pass354 · the boat

Our boat's rotten and the steward says there's nothing for a new one this cycle or next, so I'm doing it myself. Beached her in the cut, pulled six planks, scarfing in what I can get. Most of the pass on that, and no reading. I want it down that there was no reading, and why, so nobody after me thinks the wire went quiet.

Doss is dead nine years and I do the tides alone. The steward has been asked twice to put somebody else up here. She says the post is two on the book. It's been one on the water since Doss.

And I walk the vine alone, which is the part I don't like. Neru taught me that run after she'd stopped being able to feel it. She had the words and I had the feet. I'd put mine on a graft and say quiet, and she'd ask quiet how, and I'd try to tell her, and she'd say that's stirring. Neither of us ever knew whether she was right.

So that's how I have it. Learned off somebody who couldn't check me. There's been nobody to say I'm wrong for nine years, and I hang things out on it.

There's a thing in her book I found when I read the watch in. The eleventh, and the low field. I said stirring. She went down and put her own feet on the graft and wrote quiet, and put her name in the column to make it stand, and the water came over before light. She couldn't feel that graft. She says so on the same page.

She never told me. What she did was write down that she hadn't told me.

It's grazing and there was nobody on it, and I still don't know what I'd have done with it said out loud. What stays is that she put it where I'd get it, which is not the same as telling me.

Anyway. The boat floats. She's ugly. I sailed her round to Low Ford and back on the ebb and she took nothing, and sat in her a while at the mooring feeling pleased with myself. Then I came up and lit the lamp.

Before and now

New thing tonight. It makes a little holder and puts a count in it. Then you ask the holder and it gives you the count. Then it puts a different count in the same holder. Ask again. Different answer:

That's a vessel. Ask what six joined to one is. It's seven. It was seven before Maren and it'll be seven after me. This one answers differently depending on when you ask. Then it went further and I had to get the lamp trimmed. It set a fork on a vessel. Look at what's in the holder. If it's seven, put eighty-eight in. If it isn't, put ninety-nine in:

Then it ran that same line twice. Same marks, no difference, laid down the one after the other. And the two runs came out different, because the first run changed what the second run was looking at:

…then the very same line again, mark for mark, and the vessel now holding what it just set…

Say that out loud. The same words, said twice, and they don't mean the same thing the second time, because the first time changed the room. That's not arithmetic any more. Arithmetic doesn't care when you do it. Then I went down and put a chalk mark on the bench. Rubbed it out. Put it back. That's a vessel. I've been doing that with chalk my whole life and never had a word for it.

Now I have the word, and the mark for it: . Make one. Ask it. Set it. Ask it again.

Rows all season. Lay things in a line, ask for the one at the front:

Fine. Then it asked a row for something that isn't in it. It gave back a mark I haven't seen before. So I chased that mark. It matches itself. It matches nothing else. Not none, not one, not two, not any count I can put against it. It went through a good many to make the point:

…and not any count I could set against it, ever…

On the page it goes as no-answer.

Here's why I like it, and I do like it. Ask a chest for a thing that isn't in the chest and the chest tells you nothing. It just sits there, and you're standing in the store-room deciding whether you looked hard enough.

This thing answers. says not here, and says it so you can't mistake it for a count, because it won't match one. Somebody had to decide to build a mark for the absence and make sure it couldn't be taken for anything present. They built it because something downstream is going to ask a lot of questions, and some of them won't have answers.

The tireless one

It has learned to ask what kind of thing a thing is. Give it a made verb and it says yes. Give it a count and it says no:

And it works on the marks it was given at the start. Those are verbs too. I hadn't thought about that till it showed me:

Then it used that to build a sameness that looks first and asks after. Is this a doing or a thing? If they're not the same kind, they're not the same. If they're rows, compare them as rows. If they're counts, compare them as counts:

That's the first mark I've met that asks a question about the question before it answers it.

Row-craft, a long run of it, and none of it hard: how many things a row holds; the thing standing at a named place in it; setting a new thing at the head of a row; the two-way sameness above, put to a hundred rows and true in every one. Copied whole. I wanted the bench.
A two-thing row given a name of its own, and its two parts named first and second. It is the knot again in a plainer coat. Four lines, and I have them.
