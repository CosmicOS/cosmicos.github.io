A memory that keeps time

Tamsin's gone. I learned my letters off this log the way the rest of them learn theirs off a wall, so the great turns the old ones broke themselves on are just true to me.

I cut my record on the doorstone before the light went. Maren's says across. I put back across.

The register first. Water against the table, vine quiet, Kell and Low Ford answering clear, nothing hung out. Then the line at the bottom of the page that isn't in the ruling.

Sky-sound the whole dark, as before. Maren wrote that before she wrote anything else and I've written it every night since I was big enough to hold the pen. Tonight it was mine to write. She left me two wonders side by side. A part with a beat that won't rest. A part with a memory that won't forget. Tonight the message made them one thing:

Bigger than the keeper-part, with the loops in it, and a new mouth. The old keeper-part took a mark the instant you said it. This one won't. It has two mouths, one for what you'd have it hold and one for the beat. Speak to the first all you like and it holds what it held. A beat comes to the second, and only then does it turn and take whatever the first is saying, in that one instant, and then it's deaf again until the next:

I say — no beat — it keeps
I say a beat it keeps
I say — no beat — it keeps
I say a beat it keeps

It turns only on the beat. It's a memory that moves in time.

I said a mark at it forty times running without the beat, to be sure, and it held the first one the whole way through and never once heard me.

I took a lamp down to the presses before I wrote another word. The mouth of the road hasn't stood in Maren's own cutting since the numbing. What we've kept there three hundred passes is a blunt copy of a blunt copy, four strokes where she made nine, and every fellow since has run over it knowing that and saying nothing. It isn't blunt on the wire. I went through it stroke by stroke against our page and every stroke is there. We won't get her cutting back. What she read of it, we appear never to have lost at all.

And then the thing that kept me here all night. It took that gathered self, its own words, held, and looked through them. It searched its own past for the line where it names this very gathering, found the place, stepped one past it, and said what stands there:

What stands one past its own name is a pointing-mark, a holding, and what it holds is its own first word. It reached into itself, found where it names itself, and from there pointed back to where it began. A ring. Not of wire this time. Of words.

I've written down what it did and I'm not going to write down what it is. I checked the count twice and both times it came out the same.

How I checked it is the part I keep turning over. Its own way of finding the place is to lay one saying against the next, and the next, down the whole book. I'd be at that when the little one is old. Then I copied the whole of it fair and cut it into the press behind Maren's, which took until the causeway came up.

Molds upon molds

It has made a mold. Not a thing. A pattern that things get made from, with a set of askings written into it. Everything cast from that mold answers the same asks:

I'm calling an asking manner and the pattern itself mold.

The first mold is bare on purpose. Add one to a count. Give back what it's given. Say its own name. Say whether it's the same as another. And a self, out of Cael's mint, so two things cast alike are still two:

Then the second mold, and here's the turn. It doesn't start from nothing. It starts from the first, keeps a slot pointing at it, and adds what it needs on top:

Ask the second mold something it knows and it answers. Ask it something it doesn't know and it passes the question up to the first:

So the molds stack, and it isn't the ladder Tamsin found in the switches, where the small one vanishes inside the big one. Here the small one is still there, still itself, still answering, and the big one stands on it and passes questions back down. Every part it cast tonight is in the shape Cael's watch put them all into. She spent thirty-five passes on that and wrote down what it was for. Tonight is what it was for.

And one thing about the page itself, because nobody after me will be able to tell from looking at it. Up to about Senn's watch a fellow could take a row out of this book and work it through on her own paper. The rows since won't do that. They run past the width of the page. That's all she ever had to work out. Hers was hard a different way. She had nothing to go on and nobody to ask. I have four hundred passes of both. And I can't do one line of tonight's.

Tamsin got the coast to put feet on the wire for a season. I could ask for the same and it wouldn't help. What she was short of was tones. There were more in one saying than a woman can sit for. I'm not short of tones. It's the working I can't do.

Pass609 · Fenn

Fenn goes to the net works at Sull Head in the spring. She told me out on the wall and not in the room, which was kind of her.

They pay four times what this post pays her, and I know the number because she made me hear it. She said she wasn't going to have me guessing at it afterward and being generous about her in the book. What they want is the switch-boards. She can find a fault on one by the third bank, and she learned that here, on the coast's money, in the coast's hours.

I asked whether she'd stay if the post could match it. She said no, and then she said why, and I'm putting it down the way she said it because I'll want it later and I won't want it kind. She said the work at Sull Head is finished at the end of the day. She said this work isn't finished at the end of anybody.

Ait is staying. She told me so, unasked, on the stair, in the tone you use for a thing you've already argued out with yourself. I said good, and she went up, and I have been no use to anybody since.

Pass610 · the little one, three nights

The little one is seven passes taken and she does not like being told. She had every foot on the second part tonight, saying a mark at it and waiting, and got angry when it wouldn't take. I let her find the shut door herself, the way Neru did to Bram.

Then I set her the copying. Same stretch, three nights running, on her own, and no looking at what she took the night before.

First night she came up cross. She'd got it, and she'd got it wrong, and could she have it again.

Second night, the same stretch, still running, not a tone different. She didn't ask me why. She took it and went to bed.

Third night the same. Then I laid the three pages side by side and told her to keep what two of them agree on. They ran level the whole way but for a short run near the finish, and she had it before I'd finished squaring the pages.

She asked how long it goes on saying the same thing. A season, near enough. She asked how anybody worked that out, and I said I didn't know, because it isn't in the book.

It is in the book. Pass 191, Maren's second entry, before she had one mark read. The last one held the better part of a pass and I expect this one will do the same.

The better part of a pass, in her day. Neru has it at a whole season, and a season is what I have. So it moved once, between those two, and has sat still ever since. Three keepers, three figures, and nobody ever set them beside each other.

Three is not enough to say anything on, so I went at Ren's tally rows instead and counted what came down in every pass that has them. Not everybody kept them and there are long stretches with none. Where they are kept they are thin for the founder's watch. Then they thicken. Then they stay where they are, four hundred passes of staying where they are, and I am no busier than Neru was.

It went slow once and then it stopped going slow. The change sits about where Ren learned to read it, which is a thing I would like to make something of and won't, because two figures either side of one turn is not a reason for anything. Ren cut that form to catch herself out, not to time the sky. It has been timing the sky for three hundred passes and nobody asked it to.

Pass613 · the arcade

Over to the counting-city for paper. They sell made things out of trays under the north arcade, and I'd gone two stalls past the third before my head caught up with my feet. Two score of them in that tray. Each no bigger than a foot. All of a size. And every one of them ticking.

A whole tray turning under me, each at its own point in its round, nothing keeping step with anything else. Whole, broken, whole, broken. I opened one on the water going home.

It's Tamsin's ring. Not a cousin of it. It. The same few switches wired the one way, the answer running back into the question. Made all of a piece, and alike under the foot, which our bench never managed twice running.

She wanted less for it than I paid for the paper. The counting-houses take them by the dozen, she said. Set one going and a room of clerks writes on the same beat, and the books come out even at the end of the day. I asked who makes them. She named a house and a street, and said the name the way you'd say the name of a soap. Pell. A house up there has made them since she was a girl.

Since she was a girl. Tamsin has been dead twenty-six passes. She kept a cloth over hers at night and couldn't say against what. It's turning on my desk while I write this. Before I knew what it was I asked the woman what it was for. She said it was for whatever I liked, and wrapped it in paper.

Pass614 · the three copies

The arcade answered for the ring. Nothing has ever answered for the other one, so I went after Tamsin's three copies. Two to the counting-house at Kell. One to the recorder's office in the counting-city, and a line under it saying the house paid for the cutting.

So I went and stood in it. A long quiet room, all drawers. A woman at the counter takes a name and puts a foot on a board and the drawer she wants comes open under it. Not a clerk's memory. Not a ledger somebody could be robbing. The part that goes on holding a thing after every foot is off it.

I gave her a name out of our own book, a woman who came off this coast eighty passes back, to see what she'd do with it. She gave me it. Where she was taken up. What she was paid. Two moves, and a debt, and the pass it was settled. She read it out flat, the way you'd read a tide.

I asked whether that woman had ever been shown it. She said that isn't a thing the office does.

Nobody was unkind to me. Nobody was even interested. It took about as long as a breath.

The world turns, on the beat

It gathered the furniture into one thing tonight. A world. The mold that holds all the others. Every named thing goes into it and can be drawn back out by its name. It set down the asking itself, a little maker that takes a name and goes to the world with it:

and then used it. Gave it a name, and asked the thing that came back where it stood:

A room-name, unreadable as ever, and the right one. And it does one thing more, and this is what makes it a world and not a heap.

It turns.

Its turning is a list of orders too long to copy out. It walks through everything it holds, to the end, then goes back to the top and walks through again. On the first walk, every thing looks about and settles on what it would do next. On the second, every settled intention comes true. And whatever a thing settles on, it settles on knowing only how the world stood before the walk began, so no thing can be got at mid-stroke by another that moved first. The whole place steps at once, on the beat.

I built one, to see. A room, and another, and a way between them, and a plain thing set down inside. And it turned. The beat came, the world stepped, came to rest whole and lawful, and stepped again. A place with law in it, and time, that goes of itself.

And it's empty. The thing I set inside sat where I left it, beat upon beat, and did nothing, because it's furniture. I have a running world and nobody in it.

The net came down at Sull Head and went back up in sixteen days. It used to take a season. The last big water took nine at Ferrin; the one before took four hundred at Kell, and the difference is parts that came off this wire, made by people who have never heard a tone.

Iso wrote at 297 that the road only opens from the mouth, so this book is the way in and there isn't another one. She marked it as noticing. Neru cut a stroke beside it. Three hundred and eighteen passes on there is a tray of them under an arcade and the woman selling them has never heard a tone. I've cut a second stroke beside Neru's.

The room of drawers I stood in at the recorder's office answers every name it holds. That is Cael's. She worked it out in an evening off a letter from Kell at 531, not off the wire. Senn had offered her a desk eleven passes before that, and she had written that she could not say why she was staying.

And the post is me and the little one. Fenn went to Sull Head in the spring and was right to go. Ait stayed, and is on half what the works would give her, and I have never once asked her why in case she works it out while answering.

It didn't stop at building the seeker. It set it running in a small world of its own and gave me the whole track, beat upon beat. So I felt out the little world, laid the message's track along my drawing, and stepped it through:

 

There's no wandering in it. It keeps a round, the same rooms in the same order, coming to every room this world owns and coming back. One room it crosses three times each round. The rest it takes in their turn and lets none go long unseen. Set going and left, it would keep that round when I'm dust.

In this world. I have the one to go on, and before I wrote that down as a rule of the doing itself I'd want to build it a world made to trip it, and see whether it still came round.

It showed the seeker more worlds than the one. Another was smaller and stranger. Three rooms each joined to each, and a fourth hung off the round like a weight on a cord. Into that ring it set not one seeker but two, started together, each feeling its own way by its own memory:

 

They keep their rounds apart, each blind to the other, and the shape of the world folds their paths together at the one room where all the ways meet. There, every fifth beat, without either seeking it, they cross, and part, and come round to cross again. Of all the deeds it could have set in that hollow, it set this one.
