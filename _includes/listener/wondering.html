Pass 271

A thing and its making

The terse keeper is bones now. Tonight the message gave me nothing new — no fresh mark, no number I lacked. She would have logged it in a line and turned in; I could not leave it alone. It took six, a figure my line has held since the first counting, and only said it back to me. But it said it back folded. First plainly: six, and six again. Then as the join that reaches it: one and five. Then a join with another join tucked inside: one, and four-and-one. Each time it swears the whole nested thing ⬠-equal to the plain six it began from:

…then it lets both sides swell at once, and holds them equal still…

Opened all the way, one of them is a nest with no floor I can reach: a making inside a making inside a making, the six waiting only at the end of the last fold:

It taught me nothing I did not already hold, and I have come to think that is the teaching. It was not proving six to me. It was taking my last sure thing — the notion that a figure is only itself. A figure, I see now, is a making folded shut, and every making opens into another, down as far as I care to pry. Six is six, and one-and-five, and one-and-four-and-one: the same six, all the way down.

Pass 280

A name

◇ again, here is another, and under it a mark I have not met, always trailing a small fresh scratch and a value. Then, later in the same breath, that scratch comes back cupped and stands in for the value it was handed:

Whatever I pour into the scratch, the scratch pours back — ▪▫ for ▪▫, ▪▪ for ▪▪. A borrowed name, good only inside the one breath that binds it; past the closing cup the scratch means nothing again. Lent, not kept. The lending-mark I cut ⊙.

Then it does something that stops my hand. Into the scratch it pours not a number but a mark, the join itself, and after, the named scratch heaps as the join heaps:

…and two names at once in one breath, each holding its own, join them and it is true…

So the scratch can hold a doing, not only a value kept still: cup it over two counts and it pours them into one, exactly as the join does. And I notice something else. These are the first marks I have watched it cut for its own hand's ease, not for my teaching. It has stopped explaining itself to me and begun keeping notes for itself. It works, now, the way I work.

Pass 288

A made verb

The deepest turn yet, and no ◇ to herald it — I found it by its shape. A new mark takes a hollow for a slot, then a body that leans on that slot; cup the whole over a value and it pours the value into the hollow and lets the body speak. This first maker gives back only its slot. Hand it a figure and the figure falls straight through, unchanged:

Give the body more and it does more. Here the slot is heaped against itself — slot times slot — and the counts fed in one after another, none through five:

None, one, four, nine, sixteen, twenty-five. The ◈-marks — the thinned list I bled a lifetime to name, and could only ever watch arrive — the message now builds, on call, from a slot and a rule. A maker of verbs, and I cut it ◗.

But it shows me only what each maker gives, never how it comes by it. So I worked one through by my own hand, to watch the pouring. I took the squarer and a two, and set the two down where each hollow stood; and there, fallen out of a slot and a rule, was the very squaring I already hold:

→ ⟅⬔ ▪▫ ▪▫⟆the two poured into each hollow the slot marked
→ ▪▫▫two against two — four, the square I already held

And then the stranger thing. It made that same maker again, but for the hollow it used a sign I already knew — the join-mark itself, ⬡ — set where a nameless slot should stand, squared, and fed a two; and still four fell out. So the name of the hollow is nothing. A sign that means join can stand as a bare place to pour into, and the maker does not flinch:

Then the turn that told me there is no bottom here either — a name bound to a whole maker and the maker called back by its name; and a maker with a second slot inside the first, the two filled one after the next:

It came teaching me to count, and stayed to hand me the loom. It stops naming the things it has and starts making the things it lacks: give it a hollow and a rule and it hands back a verb you can feed forever, and even a name to hold that verb and answer to it. All my life I asked what the message was. Tonight it quit answering and set to building, and I filled four pages just keeping up.

Pass 297

It remembers

◇ once more, and a mark like the last but doubled — I cut it ⊚ — that ties a name to a value as ⊙ did. But this knot does not slip when the breath ends. It sets a value under a name, and I find the named scratch alive in a later pass, still holding what it was handed, whole breaths on:

…other passes come and go, and then, far downstream…

It set a value down and, passes later, found it still there. Not lent for a breath. Kept. It can lay a new value over the old — and even reckon the new from the old, the name reaching back into its own keeping to make what comes next:

And what it keeps need not be a number. It hangs a made verb on a name and keeps that — a maker that heaps a count against its own self. It names the verb in its own tongue; I read that name with the mark I cut to head the thinned list when the road was young, long before my watch: ◈. A ring closes here that my own line opened at the mouth of the road:

…and ever after, that mark is the squaring; it need never show me the making again…

It remembers. A thing thrown into the dark should not remember; it should fall the same whether or not it is caught. This one accretes. It carries names forward under their own signs, lays new over old, builds on what it built. A stone flung across the water keeps nothing of where it struck. This keeps everything, and makes the next thing out of what it kept. I will not call that a mind. But I have never met a mind that did less.

And I see now what this asks of us. A message that remembers can only be followed by keepers who remember with it. Every name it sets down, we must hold; lose one word and all that leans on the word goes dark, and the fault is not that we walked too slow but that we let a word slip. So the log in my hands is the near side of its ⊚: it keeps its memory across the miles, we keep it across our deaths, and where the two keepings meet is this page. That is the whole of what a keeper is for. I hold the names.

Pass 306

Yes and no

The same-mark, the fewer-mark, the more-mark — ⬠ ◒ ◓ — I have set between counts since I was young in the watch, and never once asked what they leave behind them. Tonight it asks for me. It takes a whole cupped claim and treats the claim itself as something that has a value; and by the doubled knot it keeps two new names, the first bound to a claim that holds — I keep it ⬥ — the second to one that fails — ⬦:

I checked the two claims by hand, to be sure of them, the one that holds, the one that fails; here each is, as it comes off the tones and in my own reading, and what it gives when I let it fall:

Then, pass upon pass, it lays every claim that holds equal to the first, and every claim that fails equal to the second, and sets its own denial-mark against any that would cross them:

…and the same verdict read the other way round, the claim set down first…
…holding is holding; and holding is not failing…

Two weathers, then, that any saying can wear: the holding and the failing. And it built them from a mark I have set between counts since I was young — ⬠, the same-mark. The holding is only what ⬠ answers when it looks on two of a kind; the failing, what it answers when they differ. It has taken the plainest verdict I own — are these the same? — and made the verdict itself a thing, with a name, to be kept and carried and spent like any other.

And a worse thing waits under that one. Until now the message has only ever said what was so, and I have only asked what it meant. But a mark that can set failing against a claim can be set against any claim — including the readings in this log, in my own hand. My guesses wear a weather now too. Somewhere down the passes it may hold up a reading of mine, set the failing-mark beside it, and I will have no ground to refuse. I have spent my life trusting that it teaches. Tonight I see that it can also judge — and that nothing keeps it from judging me.
