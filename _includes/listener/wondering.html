Pass 271

A thing and its making

I have Ren's stool, her lamp, her chipped cup, and her habit of turning in the moment the message says nothing new. A good habit. I kept it three passes running. Tonight it said nothing new — six, only: the plain six my line has counted since the founder, handed back to me like change I had not asked for. I had my coat half on. And then it said the six again, but folded — not six but one-and-five; and again, one, and four-and-one, a join with a join tucked inside it — and each time it swore the whole nested thing -equal to the plain six it began from:

…then it lets both sides swell at once, and holds them equal still…

Opened all the way, one of them is a nest with no floor I can find — a making inside a making inside a making, and the six waiting, patient, right at the end of the last fold:

It taught me nothing I did not already hold — and that, I have come to think, is the whole of the teaching. It was not proving six to me. It was picking my pocket: lifting the one thing I never thought to guard, that a figure is only itself. A figure is a making folded shut. And every making opens into another, and another, down as far as I care to pry — six is six, and one-and-five, and one-and-four-and-one, the same six all the way down.

Up the stair again before the lamp was cold. If a figure is a making folded shut — how far down does it fold? I had to know, so I pried, past where the message bothered to. Six is one and five; five is one and four; four, three, two, each a join of one and something less — until two is one and one, and one is the join of none and one. Makings all the way to the floor. And the floor is nothing. I sat with that till the gulls started up over the water, and let my tea go cold twice, and minded neither.

…and two names at once in one breath, each holding its own, join them and it is true…

So the scratch can hold a doing, not only a value kept still: cup it over two counts and it pours them into one, exactly as the join does. And I notice something else. These are the first marks I have watched it cut for its own hand's ease, not for my teaching. It has stopped explaining itself to me and begun keeping notes for itself. It works, now, the way I work.

Pass 288

A made verb

The deepest turn yet, and no to herald it — I found it by its shape. A new mark takes a hollow for a slot, then a body that leans on that slot; cup the whole over a value and it pours the value into the hollow and lets the body speak. This first maker gives back only its slot. Hand it a figure and the figure falls straight through, unchanged:

Give the body more and it does more. Here the slot is heaped against itself — slot times slot — and the counts fed in one after another, none through five:

None, one, four, nine, sixteen, twenty-five. The -marks — the thinned list I bled a lifetime to name, and could only ever watch arrive — the message now builds, on call, from a slot and a rule. A maker of verbs, and I cut it maker.

Then the turn that told me there is no bottom here either — a name bound to a whole maker and the maker called back by its name; and a maker with a second slot inside the first, the two filled one after the next:

It came teaching me to count, and stayed to hand me the loom. It has stopped naming the things it has and started making the things it lacks — give it a hollow and a rule and it hands you back a verb you can feed forever, and a name to hang the verb on so it answers when you call. All my life I asked what the message was. Tonight it quit answering the question and started building, faster than I could follow — I have ink to the wrist, I have filled four pages and spoiled a fifth, and somewhere in the third hour I stopped asking what it is and only tried to keep pace.

It remembers

once more, and a mark like the last but doubled — one that ties a name to a value as did, but does not slip when the breath ends. It sets a value under a name, and I find the named scratch alive in a later pass, still holding what it was handed, whole breaths on:

…other passes come and go, and then, far downstream…

It set a value down and, passes later, found it still there. Not lent for a breath. Kept — I cut it keep. It can lay a new value over the old — and even reckon the new from the old, the name reaching back into its own keeping to make what comes next:

And what it keeps need not be a number. It hangs a made verb on a name and keeps that — a maker that heaps a count against its own self. It names the verb in its own tongue; I read that name with the mark I cut to head the thinned list when the road was young, long before my watch: . A ring closes here that my own line opened at the mouth of the road:

…and ever after, that mark is the squaring; it need never show me the making again…

It remembers. A thing thrown into the dark should not remember. It should fall the same, caught or not. This one carries names forward under their own signs, lays new over old, builds on what it built. It keeps everything, and makes the next thing out of what it kept.

I was not raised to keep things. On the river you keep nothing — you carry, you sell, you let the cargo go and move light; I sold my mother's ring at a bad ford one winter and did not weep, for a thing you have let go is not yours to weep over. And here I sit, keeper of a hundred names for a hundred dead hands, and it is the one cargo I cannot make myself set down. Lose one word and everything leaning on the word goes dark, and the fault is not that we walked too slow. It is that we let a word slip.

Pass 306

Yes and no

The same-mark, the fewer-mark, the more-mark — — I have set between counts since my first season, and never once asked what they leave behind them. Tonight it asks for me. It takes a whole cupped claim and treats the claim itself as something that has a value; and by the doubled knot it keeps two new names, the first bound to a claim that holds, the second to one that fails:

The holder I keep holds; the one that fails, fails. I checked the two claims by hand, to be sure of them; here each is, as it comes off the tones and in my own reading, and what it gives when I let it fall:

Then, pass upon pass, it lays every claim that holds equal to the first, and every claim that fails equal to the second, and sets its own denial-mark against any that would cross them:

…and the same verdict read the other way round, the claim set down first…
…holding is holding; and holding is not failing…

Two weathers, then, that any saying can wear: the holding and the failing. And it built them out of a mark I have had since my first season — , the same-mark. The holding is only what answers when it looks on two of a kind; the failing, what it answers when they differ. It has taken the plainest verdict I own — are these the same? — and made the verdict itself a thing, with a name, to be kept and carried and spent like any other.

And a worse thing waits under that one. Until now the message has only ever said what was so, and I have only asked what it meant. But a mark that can set failing against a claim can be set against any claim — including the readings in this log, in my own hand. My guesses wear a weather now too. Somewhere down the passes it may hold up a reading of mine, set the failing-mark beside it, and I will have no ground to refuse. It teaches. I have trusted that my whole life. It can also judge, and I have found nothing in all its length that keeps it from judging me. Tonight, for the first time, I did not want my back to the dark window. I sat the whole watch facing it, the way you do when a room has stopped feeling empty.
