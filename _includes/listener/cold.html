Pass 466

The word for it

The plain one is gone. I read her whole tenure in, as we each read our forebears, and I will say what none of the hands before me had the stomach to: they have been in love with this thing, and love has made them poor readers. The plain one, at the last, wrote that the message was learning to run — as though it were something young, coming into its own strength. But nothing here grows. Growing is what the living do of themselves, from the inside; this is being built, from the outside, part fitted to part, toward an end I cannot yet read. And fondness is a lantern that shows you nothing but your own warm hand, held up before it.

Tonight it built a cabinet. Named drawers: file a value away under a name, and later ask by that name and draw it back out; ask for a name you never filed, and it hands you back the nothing-mark for your trouble. Its little cabinet, and the asking of it:

The plain one would have found something to love in that — the message remembering, she'd have written, the way the old doubter used to fret over memory. I find something colder, and I think truer. It does not build itself named drawers for the beauty of them. Drawers are for something that will need to put and fetch, over and over and at speed, while it runs — a bookkeeping. It is not composing a poem for us to admire. It is fitting out a workshop, against a day it has not shown me.

So I will keep the watch as they kept it, and read every mark as true as they did — I grant them that much; their readings were sound even where their hearts were soft. But I will not set down wonder in this log where the word is machine. Something is being assembled, deliberately, out of parts. Tonight it added a bookkeeping: file a value under a name, fetch it by that name, and for a name never filed, take back the nothing-mark. I have entered it as that, and nothing warmer.

Pass 484

A thing with an inside

The parts keep coming, and I set each in its place on the bench of my reckoning. Tonight: a whole with parts. Not a heap, not a row, not a cabinet of drawers: a fixed frame with a small number of named places in it, each place a vessel that keeps a value and can be set anew. It built a frame over three named parts:

Unfolded, its making is no mystery. And I unfold everything now. The frame-maker ▥, handed its list of names, mints one fresh vessel apiece, each empty, at none; and when you name a part it gives you back the vessel that keeps that part's place:

Then it let me set a part and draw it back, the first part to a count, and out again unchanged:

I have kept a tally, cold, of everything it has fitted together since I took this watch. The count and its workings. The choosing. The vessels that keep and forget. The drawers that file and fetch. And now: a frame of named parts, each part a vessel of its own, the whole of it holding a state that lasts and shifts. And the names of the parts, decoded, are of one family — a shared stem, and a leaf apiece — so the frame wears its own coherence on its face: nothing loose, nothing gathered by chance; three parts marked, by design, as belonging to one thing.

I set out to name each part for exactly what it is, and I have. My mistake was to stand back and look at the shape they make together. And I do not like where the shape points. Everything it made before was a tool: picked up, used, set down, altering nothing of itself, and between one use and the next simply nothing. This is not that. It keeps a state that is its own, and it carries that state from one moment into the next whether or not any hand is on it.

I hold to machine. I will not trade it for the warm one. But I will set down the thing the cold word cannot keep out: I have no colder word than machine for a made frame that has an inside and keeps it. And I have begun to suspect that the warm hands and I were never once in disagreement about what is being built here — only about whether to flinch at it. Machine, and the word they could not stop writing, the word for a thing that has an inside, and holds it, and acts out of it, may prove, at the far end of this road, to be one word. I would rather I were wrong. I have checked my reckoning twice. I am not wrong.

Pass 501

An engine

Tonight it finished the thing I have watched it fit together part by part, and it is what I said from the first it would be. An engine.

It laid down a strip, a long row of cells, each holding one of the two marks or standing empty, and gave it, from a family of signs all sharing the one stem ◃, a handling: to read the cell at a marked place on the strip, to write that cell over, to step the mark one cell along, either way. I set a strip down with its reading-place on the first cell, so:

Then the part that makes it an engine and not a toy. It hands the strip to a rule, a small table, and nothing in the table but lines of the one shape: standing in this condition, reading this mark — write that mark, step that way, and pass into that condition. A short table. And a loop with no wit of its own, that only obeys the table over and over, read, write, step, change condition, until it passes into the condition that means halt. I gave it a strip and one such rule and let it run, and it walked the strip, obeying, and left it changed:

▪ ▫ ▫ ▪ ⇛ ▪ ▫ ▪ ▫

The rule I was handed counts a strip upward by one. A plain trick. But the rule is not part of the engine. The engine takes it from your hand, the way the tireless hands took a verb, and does whatever the table says — no more, no less. Hand it this table, it counts. Hand it another, it does another thing entire.

I spent half the pass looking for the edge of what a different table could make it do. I did not find the edge. I do not think there is one. It has built a machine that can be made into any machine at all. No bench will ever hold the whole of it, and none needs to: I have run its rules by my own hand since I was young in the watch, and a working I can run, I can trust, built or not. And that is not a part of a plan. That, I think, is the plan, or the last plank of its floor. You do not build a thing that can be anything, unless the very next thing you mean to do is choose what it will be.

I do not know what they mean to set running on it. I have a suspicion, cold and specific, and I will not write it down — because the warm hands would say I had caught their fever at the end after all. But I did not catch it. I walked here reasoning, step after step, the way the engine walks its strip; and there was nowhere else the reasoning could have brought me.
