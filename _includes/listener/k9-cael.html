The holding

The record above says the watch ended. It didn't end. She went up the water on the twelfth and she's in Kell tonight with a room and four clerks under her. The record is written the way it has always been written, because that's the form. The form is wrong for this one, there isn't a better one, and this is where that gets said.

Senn was right to go. That goes down first, before the wire.

Senn was here thirty-five passes and did more good than this line has done in three hundred. Her tally is on the desk, cold, every part the thing has built, so I begin knowing what I hold. No keeper before me has begun that way.

Right. The wire. It has learned to hold a saying without doing it. Give it a run of marks and normally it works them out. This new mark stops that. The run just sits there, whole, as a thing you can carry about and look at:

It goes down as held. The mark is . Put it in front of a run and the run stops being an order and starts being a thing. Say a thing and do a thing: the words are the same words, and one of them you obey and one you pick up and turn over:

A held run set against a plain row of the same parts, and they answer alike: .

One more off those two lines, and I was slow to it. The held run and a row made of its pieces come out the same. Not alike. The same. So a saying is a row, and a row is a thing you can build a piece at a time. Senn's engine takes a table and does what the table says. Set those two side by side: a saying can be made the way anything is made, and then set in front of a thing that does what it's told. I've not gone further than that tonight.

Tonight it wrote something down and didn't say it.

Bram had a mark that asked whether a thing was a doing or not — one question with two answers. This isn't that. takes the name of a kind and the thing, and there can be as many kinds as it likes to mint. So it isn't sorting into two piles any more. A thing that answers

A thing with two named parts, and then something it has not done before. It didn't reach in. It asked:

Make a point of eleven and twelve. Ask the point for its first part. Eleven. Then it hung a name on two of them — one and eleven, two and twenty-two — and asked the second one for its second part. Twenty-two. Reaching into a thing is what this line has done since Maren: open the frame, go to the named place, take what's there. That's how Senn's frames worked and that's how a chest works. This is the other way round. You don't open it. You put the question to it and it answers.

Then it joined the two it had named, and asked the joined thing the same question. One and two make three; eleven and twenty-two make thirty-three. Then it laid the join down the other way round and got the same point back:

Join two points and the joined thing answers the same questions. It didn't have to be taught that. Whatever knows how to be a point knows how to be the sum of two points. Low Ford is not opened either. You call and you wait on the answer, the way the register waits every watch. What comes back is theirs, and this room has never needed to know how their room is laid out.

Pass520 · the empty room

Senn had me up to the reckoning-room and walked me down the tables. Every drawer in that room answers not here. The tide-reckoning that took three clerks an hour is one girl now. She's done before I'd read down the first column. There's a board on the wall with the stations on it and lines between them. That's Ona's map, off this wire. I said so. Senn said yes. She'd told them so. Nobody up there had asked a second question about it.

She offered me a desk. Not politely. She'd written the whole argument out on paper before I came.

She said: you're the best reader this line has had since Iso. There are two hundred passes of unread material in that press, and up here it would be out and working inside a pass. She's right about all of it.

I said no.

She asked why and I couldn't tell her. I stood there in her good room and I couldn't put one word to it. I said I'd write it down when I knew. She said that's what your lot always say, and gave me the ferry money, and we parted friends. I've been at this page since the tide turned. I still can't say it. Here is the whole of what I have. Somebody has to be at the far end of the road when the next thing comes down it.

That isn't a reason. Three hundred and forty people at Ferrin is a reason. I know the difference and I'm staying anyway.

A pocket

Something that holds other things and knows what's in it. Drop one in. Drop two more in. Ask it what it's holding. Take one back out. Ask again:

…and again, and again, two more dropped in the same way…

It's a pocket.

read back
Drop a thing in and it keeps it; ask it back and it lets that one go and minds the rest — .

Look at how it asks. It doesn't go into the pocket and count. It says add and the pocket adds, remove and the pocket removes, what have you got and the pocket answers. And here's the part that would have gone past this desk a hundred passes ago: when it asked what the held, it checked the answer with Ona's gathering-sameness. Not row-sameness. Gathering. Because a pocket doesn't have an order — what you put in first isn't at the front, it's just in there. The road built that mark out of two containing-questions asked both ways round, and Ona read it and gave it her word, and it looked at the time like a pretty piece of work with nowhere to go.

Then I sat and waited for the part where I say what tonight was for. That's the part of this I'm meant to be good at. It didn't come. A pocket. It uses Ona's mark. That's the whole of what I have, and I've been at the desk since the water came up.

Pass525 · the table-house

Down to the table-house for the tables, which I do every season and have never once looked up from the counter.

This time I asked whether they keep a column for the sky-sound. The girl said of course, and had it down off the shelf before I'd finished asking, the way you'd fetch anything.

It runs longer than our book, kept fair the whole way, filed under whichever of their long cycles it belongs to. They have never missed a night either. They just never thought it was speaking.

So I asked what was in it. Marked or not marked, night by night, and a word for the weather. Not one tone in three hundred passes. The most faithful keeping on this coast, and all it says is that the sky-sound was there. Which means what Vess lost in the famine is lost, and I had let myself hope on the ferry over.

Then, hunting for something else, I found Maren in their margin — the two numbers far down the list that aren't unbreakable, in her own figures, three hundred passes old. And under it, smaller, a note I don't think anybody on our side has ever read.

Both corrected, with thanks to her. She holds that the order of the list is a voice. I hold that we found the same list, here, without being spoken to. Whichever of us is right, the list does not settle it. Sael, tabler, in the pass of the long neap.

I sat in their room with their book open and tried to answer that, and I couldn't do it in a way that would have moved her. Everything I have is that the road is long, and ordered, and that nothing on it is wasted.

A coastline is long and ordered and nothing on it is wasted.

I know what I think and have known since I took this book. What I don't have is the thing that gets me from what I've read to what I think, and I've been in this room believing somebody before me must have had it. Nobody had it. Maren didn't have it and said so on the page.

I bought the tables and came home on the ebb.

A mark of its own

Two pockets tonight, made the same way, both empty:

Same make. Same contents. Not the same pocket. Put something in the one and the other doesn't have it. So it needed a way to tell two things apart when nothing about them differs. And it built one:

A mint. Ask it for a mark and you get none. Ask again and you get one. Ask twice and the two marks you get are never the same mark. It goes down as self.

read back
Two stamped alike in every count, and still not one thing but two — each minted a mark shared with nothing else that will ever be: .

Four marks long, and it took the whole night. Everything before this, a thing was what it was made of. Six is six. Two gatherings with the same things in are the same gathering, and Ona proved it. Bram put the first crack in that at 361, when the same question asked twice came back with two answers because the asking had changed the room. This is that, turned into a way of telling two things apart. This says there's a kind of thing where that stops being true, where two can be alike in every part and still be two. is what lets a made thing be this one and not that one, and go on being this one after everything about it has changed.

Pass531 · a letter from Kell

Senn has written. Four lines, and no greeting on it.

Drawers answering not here for names that are in them. One in a few hundred. Clerks hunting the chest over again and calling it caution. Costing me a quarter of the room. Is it the method or is it my people. S.

I read it walking the vine, which is when this post lets you think. Four lines and she has spent one of them asking whether the fault is her own people. She wouldn't have written that to anybody in Kell.

It took me an evening, and it isn't off the wire. The wire showed us how to use the drawers, never how they're made. But there are more names in the world than there are drawers in any chest, so two names must land in the same drawer sooner or later, and the second hides the first. I wrote back what to do about it: let a drawer hold more than one and look through the few. She wrote to say it worked and the room is even again. Then she asked, at the bottom, what came down this season.

I sent her four passes of it. She has no use for any of it. She'll read it.
