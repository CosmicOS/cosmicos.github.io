Pass 505

The holding

The record above says the watch ended. It didn't end. She went up the water on the eleventh. She's in Kell tonight with a room and four clerks under her. I've written the record the way it's always been written because that's the form. I want it said here that the form is wrong for this one and I don't know a better one. Senn was right to go. I'm putting that down first because I'm going to be tempted to unsay it later.

Three hundred and forty people at Ferrin. Nothing in this book comes near it. Not Maren's primes, not Ren's figures, not the whole of Vess's watch. Senn was here thirty-five passes and did more good than this line has done in three hundred.

Right. The wire. It has learned to hold a saying without doing it. Give it a run of marks and normally it works them out. This new mark stops that. The run just sits there, whole, as a thing you can carry about and look at:

I'm going to call it held. The mark is . Put it in front of a run and the run stops being an order and starts being a thing. What's the difference. Say a thing and do a thing. The words are the same words. One of them you obey and one of them you hold in your hand and turn over. Tonight it wrote something down and didn't say it. It made a thing out of its own speech.

Pass 511

A whole tongue

I came up meaning to spend the pass on the holding and it took the pass off me. It wants to know what kind of thing a thing is. Not what it equals. What kind:

Ten is a count. Some other thing it built isn't a count. It says so plainly. And it says which kind it's asking about.

Call that kind.

Bram had a mark that asked whether a thing was a doing or not. That was one question with two answers. This isn't that. takes the name of a kind and the thing. There can be as many kinds as it likes to mint. So it isn't sorting things into two piles any more. It's naming families, and it can name a new family whenever it wants one. I sat here trying to work out why that went through me the way it did. I've got it now.

Pass 517

A thing that answers

A thing with two named parts, and then something I've not seen it do. It didn't reach in. It asked:

Make a point of eleven and twelve. Ask the point for its first part. Eleven. I've been reaching into things since I could read. Open the frame, go to the named place, take what's there. That's how Senn's frames worked and that's how a chest works. This is the other way round. You don't open it. You put the question to it and it answers. And then two of them together:

Join two points and the joined thing answers the same questions. It didn't have to be taught that. Whatever knows how to be a point knows how to be the sum of two points. Reaching in, the knowing is in my hand. I have to know where the part sits. Asking, the knowing is in the thing. It knows where its own parts sit and I don't have to.

Pass 520 · the empty room

Over to Kell for the quarter's business and Senn had me up to the reckoning-room. It's a long room with north light and nine desks. She has the whole floor of it now.

She walked me down the tables. Every drawer in that room answers not here. The tide-reckoning that took three clerks an hour is one girl now. She's done before I'd read down the first column. There's a board on the wall with the stations on it and lines between them. That's Ona's map, off this wire at pass 455. I said so. Senn said yes. She'd told them so. Nobody up there had asked a second question about it.

She offered me a desk. Not politely. She'd written the whole argument out on paper before I came.

She said: you're the best reader this line has had since Iso. There are two hundred passes of unread material in that press. Up here it would be out and working inside a year.

She's right about all of it. I checked her arithmetic on the walk down and it's sound.

I said no.

She asked why and I couldn't tell her. I stood there in her good room and I couldn't put one word to it. I said I'd write it down when I knew. She said that's what your lot always say, and gave me the ferry money, and we parted friends. I've been at this page since the tide turned. I still can't say it. Here is the whole of what I have. Somebody has to be at the far end of the road when the next thing comes down it.

That isn't a reason. Three hundred and forty people at Ferrin is a reason. I know the difference and I'm staying anyway.

Pass 522

A pocket

Something that holds other things and knows what's in it. Drop one in. Drop two more in. Ask it what it's holding. Take one back out. Ask again:

…and again, and again, two more dropped in the same way…

It's a pocket.

read back
Drop a thing in and it keeps it; ask it back and it lets that one go and minds the rest — .

Look at how it asks. It doesn't go into the pocket and count. It says add to the pocket and the pocket adds. It says remove and the pocket removes. It says what have you got and the pocket answers. And here's the part I'd have missed a hundred passes ago. When it asked what the pocket held, it checked the answer with Ona's heap-sameness. Not row-sameness. Heap.

Because a pocket doesn't have an order. What you put in first isn't at the front. It's just in there. Ona built that mark out of two containing-questions asked both ways round. I remember thinking it was a pretty piece of work with nowhere to go.

Pass 528

A mark of its own

Two pockets tonight, made the same way, both empty:

Same make. Same contents. Not the same pocket. Put something in the one and the other doesn't have it. So it needed a way to tell two things apart when nothing about them differs. And it built one:

A mint. Ask it for a mark and you get none. Ask again and you get one. Ask twice and the two marks you get are never the same mark. I'm putting it down as self.

read back
Two stamped alike in every count, and still not one thing but two — each minted a mark shared with nothing else that will ever be: .

It's four marks long and I've been at it all night. Everything before this, a thing was what it was made of. Six is six. Two heaps with the same things in are the same heap. Ona proved that and it's true. This says there's a kind of thing where that stops being true. Where two can be alike in every part and still be two. Say it plain. It has built a way for a made thing to be this one and not that one. And to go on being this one after everything about it has changed.

Pass 531 · a letter from Kell

Senn has written with a question. Not a friendly one. A working one, four lines, and no greeting on it. I read it walking the vine, which is when this post lets you think. Her drawers are answering not here for names that are plainly in them. One in a few hundred. Her clerks have been re-hunting by hand and calling it caution. It's costing her a quarter of the room.

It took me an evening, and it isn't off the wire. The wire showed us how to use the drawers, never how they're made. But there are more names in the world than there are drawers in any chest. There have to be. So two names must land in the same drawer sooner or later. When they do, the second one hides the first. Senn sent Kell a page and a half on the using, which is all she had. I wrote back what to do about it. Let a drawer hold more than one and look through the few. She wrote to say it worked and that the room is even again. Then she asked, at the bottom, what came down this quarter.

I sent her four passes of it. She has no use for any of it. She'll read it.

Pass 534

The vessel's coat

It has gone back for the vessels. Bram found them in pass 361. A little holder. Put a count in, ask it, put a different count in, ask again. Nothing on this road is older or plainer. Tonight it made them again, in the new way, and they answer instead of being reached into:

Make one holding fifteen. Ask it. Fifteen. Tell it eighty-two. Ask it. Eighty-two.

It does exactly what Bram's did. Not nearly. Exactly. I laid her rows against these and they answer alike in every case I could put to them.

So this pass gave us nothing new, and I sat up with it till the lamp went. It's re-forging. It has two hundred passes of parts behind it. It's going back through them and putting every one into the same shape. The shape where you ask a thing instead of opening it. Nobody re-forges a whole stock for the pleasure of it. You do it before you build something that needs every part to fit the same way round.

Pass 540

The house

It built a room:

Then a door, and a door is a thing that joins two rooms, and it works from either side. Stand in the hall, go through, you're in the kitchen. Stand in the kitchen, go through the same door, you're in the hall:

Then three more doors, off the hall, and one of them goes up:

…and the shape they make, as I drew it to hold in my head — the middle, three off it, and one leading on and up:…

I drew it out to hold it in my head. A middle, three off the middle, and one leading on and up:

It's a dwelling. A hall, a kitchen, a lawn, a stair, and a room at the top of the stair. I want to be careful. I don't know that those are the words. The names are long runs I can't read. I've called them hall and kitchen because of where they sit and what joins to what. The shape is the shape whatever the names are. Then it put something in it:

It made a thing and it set the thing on the stair. I have been at this desk since the causeway went under. The lamp is nearly out and I'm going to write the rest badly. Three hundred and fifty passes. Counts, then claims, then choosing, then vessels, then rows, then heaps, then kinds, then things that answer, then a mint so that two things alike are still two. And then a place. With rooms. And doors that work both ways. And something standing in it.

Senn said workshop. It isn't a workshop. A workshop is where you make the thing. This is where you put it. I took my hands off the drawing and sat with them in my lap a while. I haven't done that since I was in the shoal.
